How it is decided: no database records peer review, so it is inferred from publication type and venue.

- The evidence is ranked PubMed > Europe PMC > DOAJ (lists only peer-reviewed journals) > OpenAlex (looked up by DOI) > Semantic Scholar. The evidence used for each work is stored in `peer_review_basis`.
- A work counts as peer reviewed if any version of it was published as a journal article or review. A preprint merged with its published version is therefore `true`.
- Preprints are recognised by DOI prefix or server name (bioRxiv incl. `10.64898`, medRxiv, Research Square, SSRN, …).
- Meeting abstracts are recognised by supplement DOIs and numbered titles such as "P1‐172: …", because OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar often type them as articles.

Caveats:

- `true` is not a quality judgement; a few venues are questionable.
- Full IEEE/EMBC proceedings papers are usually refereed but are counted `false` here, which is conservative.
- `unknown` works (mostly theses or foreign-language items without a DOI) are `false`.
- Title-based deduplication can miss garbled titles, e.g. "Aβ" written as "Ab" or "A b". Check for these during screening.
