UNLABELLED: Over the last decade, [[grafo/termos/striatal cholinergic interneurons|striatal cholinergic interneurons]] (ChIs) have reemerged as key actors in the pathophysiology of basal-ganglia-related [[grafo/termos/movement disorders|movement disorders]]. However, the [[grafo/termos/mechanisms involved|mechanisms involved]] are still unclear. In this study, we address the role of ChI activity in the expression of parkinsonian-like [[grafo/termos/motor deficits|motor deficits]] in a unilateral nigrostriatal [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-hydroxydopamine]] (6-OHDA) lesion model using optogenetic and pharmacological approaches. Dorsal striatal photoinhibition of ChIs in lesioned ChAT(cre/cre) mice expressing [[grafo/termos/halorhodopsin|halorhodopsin]] in ChIs reduces [[grafo/termos/akinesia|akinesia]], [[grafo/termos/bradykinesia|bradykinesia]], and sensorimotor neglect. [[grafo/termos/muscarinic acetylcholine receptor|Muscarinic acetylcholine receptor]] (mAChR) blockade by scopolamine produces similar anti-parkinsonian effects. To decipher which of the mAChR subtypes provides these beneficial effects, systemic and intrastriatal administration of the selective M1 and M4 mAChR antagonists telenzepine and tropicamide, respectively, were tested in the same model of Parkinson's disease. The two compounds alleviate 6-OHDA lesion-induced motor deficits. Telenzepine produces its beneficial effects by blocking postsynaptic M1 mAChRs expressed on [[grafo/termos/medium spiny neurons|medium spiny neurons]] (MSNs) at the origin of the indirect striatopallidal and direct striatonigral pathways. The anti-parkinsonian effects of tropicamide were almost completely abolished in mutant lesioned mice that lack M4 mAChRs specifically in [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] D1-receptor-expressing neurons, suggesting that postsynaptic M4 mAChRs expressed on direct MSNs mediate the antiakinetic action of tropicamide. The present results show that altered cholinergic transmission via M1 and M4 mAChRs of the [[grafo/termos/dorsal striatum|dorsal striatum]] plays a pivotal role in the occurrence of [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptoms]] in Parkinson's disease. SIGNIFICANCE STATEMENT: The [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]], where dopaminergic and cholinergic systems interact, is the pivotal structure of [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia|basal ganglia]] involved in pathophysiological changes underlying Parkinson's disease. Here, using optogenetic and pharmacological approaches, we investigated the involvement of striatal cholinergic interneurons (ChIs) and [[grafo/termos/muscarinic receptors|muscarinic receptor]] subtypes (mAChRs) in the occurrence of a [[grafo/termos/wide range|wide range]] of motor deficits such as akinesia, bradykinesia, [[grafo/termos/motor coordination|motor coordination]], and sensorimotor neglect after unilateral nigrostriatal [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine lesion|6-hydroxydopamine lesion]] in mice. Our results show that photoinhibition of ChIs in the dorsal striatum and pharmacological blockade of muscarinic receptors, specifically postsynaptic M1 and M4 mAChRs, alleviate lesion-induced motor deficits. The present study points to these receptor subtypes as potential targets for the symptomatic treatment of parkinsonian-like motor symptoms.
