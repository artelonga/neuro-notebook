The complexity of the brain, in which different [[grafo/termos/neuronal cell|neuronal cell]] types are interspersed and complexly interconnected, has posed a major obstacle in identifying [[grafo/termos/pathophysiological mechanisms|pathophysiological mechanisms]] underlying prevalent [[grafo/termos/neurological disorders|neurological disorders]]. This is largely based in the inability of classical experimental approaches to target defined [[grafo/termos/neural population|neural populations]] at sufficient temporal and [[grafo/termos/spatial resolution|spatial resolution]]. As a consequence, effective clinical therapies for prevalent neurological disorders are largely lacking. Recently developed optogenetic probes are genetically expressed photosensitive [[grafo/termos/ion channels|ion channels]] and pumps that in principal overcome these limitations. Optogenetic probes allow millisecond resolution functional control over selected optogenetically transduced [[grafo/termos/neuronal populations|neuronal populations]] targeted based on promoter activity. This optical cell control scheme has already been applied to answer fundamental questions pertaining to neurological disorders by allowing researchers to experimentally intercept, or induce, pathophysiological neuronal signaling activity in a highly controlled manner. Offering high [[grafo/termos/temporal resolution|temporal resolution]] control over [[grafo/termos/neural activity|neural activity]] at high cellular specificity, optogenetic tools constitute a game changer in research aiming at understanding pathophysiological signaling mechanisms in neurological disorders and in developing [[grafo/termos/therapeutic strategies|therapeutic strategies]] to correct these. In this regard, recent experimental work has provided new insights in [[grafo/termos/underlying mechanisms|underlying mechanisms]], as well as preliminary proof-of-principle for optogenetic therapies, of several neurological disorders, including Parkinson's disease, [[grafo/termos/epilepsy|epilepsy]] and progressive blindness. This review synthesizes experimental work where optogenetic tools have been applied to explore pathologic [[grafo/termos/neural network|neural network]] activity in models of neurological disorders.
