[[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|Deep brain stimulation]] (DBS) implantation surgery is an established treatment modality for a variety of medical refractory [[grafo/termos/movement disorders|movement disorders]] such as Parkinson's disease, [[grafo/termos/essential tremor|essential tremors]] and [[grafo/termos/dystonia|dystonia]]. Following the success of DBS in these movement disorders with a high rate of safety and efficacy, there is a resurgence of interest in the utility of this modality in other medical refractory disorders. Consequently, [[grafo/termos/neuromodulation|neuromodulation]] has been explored for a variety of refractory conditions such as [[grafo/termos/neuropsychiatric disorders|neuropsychiatric disorders]] (major depressive disorders, [[grafo/termos/obsessive-compulsive disorder|obsessive-compulsive disorders]], [[grafo/termos/addiction|addictions]]), eating disorders including obesity, traumatic [[grafo/termos/brain injury|brain injury]], post-traumatic stress disorders (PTSD), dementias and [[grafo/termos/chronic pain|chronic pain]]. This review provides an overview of the emerging applications of DBS in these disorders, including summary of the published literature. We have highlighted the pathophysiology and likely aberrant [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuits]] involved in these refractory disorders. Current and possible surgical targets for neurosurgical intervention related to these disorders have also been discussed. Furthermore, recent advances such as closed loop systems; responsive [[grafo/termos/neurostimulation|neurostimulation]] systems and optogenetics techniques have been addressed.
