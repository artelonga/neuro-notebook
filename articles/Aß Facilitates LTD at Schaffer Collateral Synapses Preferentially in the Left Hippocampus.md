Promotion of [[grafo/termos/long-term depression|long-term depression]] (LTD) mechanisms by synaptotoxic soluble oligomers of [[grafo/termos/amyloid-β|amyloid-β]] (Aß) has been proposed to underlie [[grafo/termos/synaptic dysfunction|synaptic dysfunction]] in Alzheimer's disease (AD). Previously, LTD was induced by relatively non-specific [[grafo/termos/electrical stimulation|electrical stimulation]]. Exploiting optogenetics, we studied LTD using a more physiologically diffuse spatial pattern of selective pathway activation in the rat [[grafo/termos/hippocampus|hippocampus]] in vivo. This relatively sparse synaptic LTD requires both the [[grafo/termos/ion channels|ion channel]] function and GluN2B subunit of the [[grafo/termos/nmda receptor|NMDA receptor]] but, in contrast to electrically induced LTD, is not facilitated by boosting endogenous muscarinic [[grafo/termos/acetylcholine|acetylcholine]] or metabotropic [[grafo/termos/glutamate|glutamate]] 5 [[grafo/termos/receptor activation|receptor activation]]. Although in the absence of Aß, there is no evidence of hippocampal LTD asymmetry, in the presence of Aß, the induction of LTD is preferentially enhanced in the left hippocampus in an mGluR5-dependent manner. This circuit-selective disruption of [[grafo/termos/synaptic plasticity|synaptic plasticity]] by Aß provides a route to understanding the development of aberrant brain lateralization in AD.
