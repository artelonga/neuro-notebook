OBJECTIVE: To establish optogenetic control system of phasic dopamine release in the striatum of mice in vivo. BACKGROUND: Deep brain stimulation (DBS) is a therapeutic option for medication refractory symptoms of movement disorders, including Parkinson’s disease. Traditional electrical stimulation, however, spreads surrounding structures, and may excite or inhibit all affected neurons and axons. Optogenetics is a new technology to control cell activity by combining genetic and optics. By introducing light-gated ion channels in the target neuron, turning specific neurons on or off rapidly in response to diverse colors of light is capable. Utilizing optogentics may contribute to neuron-specific and less side-effect stimulation for neuromodulation. DESIGN/METHODS: AAV-EF1a-hChR-eYFP was injected into the substantia nigra (SN) of THcre mouse. An optical fiber was positioned at the SN to deliver light. A carbon fiber electrode was implanted in the striatum and an Ag/AgCl reference electrode was positioned in the contralateral hemisphere. Optic stimulation by LED was delivered (470nm, 2ms, 60Hzx24 pulses) and striatal dopaminergic response was measured by using Fastscan cyclic voltammetry (FSCV). After the recording of response of optical stimulation, a pair of bipolar stimulating electrodes was implanted into the ipsilateral medial forebrain bundle (MFB) and electrical stimulation was applied (30Hz x24 pulses). RESULTS: Optical stimulation of MFB dopamine neurons induced striatal dopaminergic release detected by FSCV, as well as electrical stimulation did. Dopaminergic release was induced by optical stimulation depended on pulse width (1-5ms), frequency (15-60Hz), Light power (0.4-1.4mW), and depth of electrode (4.375-4.825mm). CONCLUSIONS: We demonstrated that optical stimulation induced striatal dopaminergic release, as well as electrical stimulation did. Our data suggest that optogenetics can be applied to neuromodulation instead of traditional electrical stimulation although there are many unknown parameter for better control of neuronal circuit using this new technology.
