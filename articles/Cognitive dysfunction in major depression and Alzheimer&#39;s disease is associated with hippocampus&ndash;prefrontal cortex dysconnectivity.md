Dayalan Sampath,1 Monica Sathyanesan,1,2 Samuel S Newton1,2 1Division of Basic Biomedical Sciences, Sanford School of Medicine, University of South Dakota, Vermillion, 2Sioux Falls VA Healthcare System, Sioux Falls, SD, USA Abstract: [[grafo/termos/cognitive dysfunction|Cognitive dysfunction]] is prevalent in [[grafo/termos/psychiatric disorders|psychiatric disorders]]. Deficits are observed in multiple domains, including [[grafo/termos/working memory|working memory]], [[grafo/termos/executive function|executive function]], attention, and [[grafo/termos/information processing|information processing]]. Disability caused by cognitive dysfunction is frequently as debilitating as the prominent emotional disturbances. Interactions between the [[grafo/termos/hippocampus|hippocampus]] and the [[grafo/termos/prefrontal cortex|prefrontal cortex]] are increasingly appreciated as an important link between [[grafo/termos/cognition|cognition]] and emotion. Recent developments in optogenetics, imaging, and connectomics can enable the investigation of this circuit in a manner that is relevant to disease pathophysiology. The goal of this review is to [[grafo/termos/shed light|shed light]] on the contributions of this circuit to cognitive dysfunction in [[grafo/termos/neuropsychiatric disorders|neuropsychiatric disorders]], focusing on Alzheimer&rsquo;s disease and [[grafo/termos/depression|depression]]. Keywords: hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, cognition, depression, Alzheimer&rsquo;s disease
