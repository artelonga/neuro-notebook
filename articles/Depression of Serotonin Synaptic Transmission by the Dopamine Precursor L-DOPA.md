Imbalance between the [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] and serotonin (5-HT) [[grafo/termos/neurotransmitter systems|neurotransmitter systems]] has been implicated in the comorbidity of Parkinson's disease (PD) and [[grafo/termos/psychiatric disorders|psychiatric disorders]]. [[grafo/termos/l-dopa|L-DOPA]], the leading treatment of PD, facilitates the production and release of dopamine. This study assessed the action of L-DOPA on monoamine [[grafo/termos/synaptic transmission|synaptic transmission]] in mouse [[grafo/termos/brain slices|brain slices]]. Application of L-DOPA augmented the D2-receptor-mediated inhibitory postsynaptic current (IPSC) in [[grafo/termos/dopamine neurons|dopamine neurons]] of the [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra|substantia nigra]]. This augmentation was largely due to [[grafo/termos/dopamine release|dopamine release]] from 5-HT terminals. Selective optogenetic stimulation of 5-HT terminals evoked dopamine release, producing D2-receptor-mediated IPSCs following treatment with L-DOPA. In the dorsal raphe, L-DOPA produced a long-lasting [[grafo/termos/depression|depression]] of the 5-HT1A-receptor-mediated IPSC in 5-HT neurons. When D2 receptors were expressed in the dorsal raphe, application of L-DOPA resulted in a D2-receptor-mediated IPSC. Thus, treatment with L-DOPA caused ectopic dopamine release from 5-HT terminals and a loss of 5-HT-mediated synaptic transmission.
