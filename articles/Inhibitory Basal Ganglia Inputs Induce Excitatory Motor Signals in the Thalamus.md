[[grafo/termos/basal ganglia|Basal ganglia]] (BG) circuits orchestrate complex [[grafo/termos/motor behaviors|motor behaviors]] predominantly via [[grafo/termos/inhibitory synaptic|inhibitory synaptic]] outputs. Although these inhibitory BG outputs are known to reduce the excitability of postsynaptic target neurons, precisely how this change impairs motor performance remains [[grafo/termos/poorly understood|poorly understood]]. Here, we show that optogenetic photostimulation of inhibitory BG inputs from the [[grafo/termos/globus pallidus|globus pallidus]] induces a surge of [[grafo/termos/action potentials|action potentials]] in the ventrolateral thalamic (VL) neurons and muscle contractions during the post-inhibitory period. Reduction of the [[grafo/termos/neuronal populations|neuronal population]] with this post-inhibitory rebound firing by knockout of T-type Ca2+ channels or photoinhibition abolishes multiple motor responses induced by the inhibitory BG input. In a low [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] state, the number of VL neurons showing post-inhibitory firing increases, while reducing the number of active VL neurons via photoinhibition of BG input, effectively prevents Parkinson disease (PD)-like [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptoms]]. Thus, BG [[grafo/termos/inhibitory input|inhibitory input]] generates excitatory motor signals in the [[grafo/termos/thalamus|thalamus]] and, in excess, promotes PD-like motor abnormalities.
