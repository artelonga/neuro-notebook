[[grafo/termos/dopamine|Dopamine]] (DA) profoundly stimulates [[grafo/termos/motor function|motor function]] as demonstrated by the hypokinetic [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptoms]] in Parkinson's disease (PD) and by the hyperkinetic motor side effects during dopaminergic treatment of PD. Dopamine (DA) receptor-bypassing, optogenetics- and chemogenetics-induced spike firing of striatal DA D1 receptor (D1R)-expressing, [[grafo/termos/direct pathway|direct pathway]] [[grafo/termos/medium spiny neurons|medium spiny neurons]] (dSPNs or dMSNs) promotes movements. However, the endogenous D1R-mediated effects, let alone those of DA replacement, on dSPN spike activity in [[grafo/termos/freely moving|freely-moving]] animals is not established. Here we show that using [[grafo/termos/transcription factor|transcription factor]] Pitx3 null mutant (Pitx3Null) mice as a model for severe and consistent DA denervation in the [[grafo/termos/dorsal striatum|dorsal striatum]] in Parkinson's disease, antidromically identified striatonigral neurons (D1R-expressing dSPNs) had a lower baseline spike [[grafo/termos/firing rate|firing rate]] than that in DA-intact normal mice, and these neurons increased their spike firing more strongly in Pitx3Null mice than in WT mice in response to injection of [[grafo/termos/l-dopa|L-dopa]] or the D1R agonist, SKF81297; the increase in spike firing temporally coincided with the motor-stimulating effects of L-dopa and SKF81297. Taken together, these results provide the first evidence from freely moving animals that in parkinsonian [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]], identified behavior-promoting dSPNs become hyperactive upon the administration of L-dopa or a D1 agonist, likely contributing to the profound dopaminergic motor stimulation in [[grafo/termos/parkinsonian animals|parkinsonian animals]] and PD patients.
