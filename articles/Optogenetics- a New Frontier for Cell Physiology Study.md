Optogenetics is the combination of optical and molecular strategies to control designated molecular and cellular activities in [[grafo/termos/living tissues|living tissues]] and cells using [[grafo/termos/genetically encoded|genetically encoded]] [[grafo/termos/light-sensitive proteins|light-sensitive proteins]]. It involves the use of light to rapidly gate the membrane channels that allows for ion movement. Optogenetics began with the placing of light-sensitive proteins from green algae inside specific types of brain cells. The cells can then be turned on or off with pulses of blue and yellow light. Using the naturally occurring algal protein [[grafo/termos/channelrhodopsin-2|Channelrhodopsin-2]] (ChR2), a rapidly gated [[grafo/termos/light sensitive|light-sensitive]] [[grafo/termos/cation channels|cation channel]], the number and frequency of [[grafo/termos/action potentials|action potentials]] can be controlled. The ChR2 provides a way to manipulate a single type of neuron while affecting no others, an unprecedented specificity. This technology allows the use of light to alter neural processing at the level of single spikes and synaptic events, yielding a widely applicable tool for neuroscientists and biomedical engineers. An improbable combination of green algae, lasers, [[grafo/termos/gene therapy|gene therapy]] and fiber optics made it possible to map [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuits]] deep inside the brain with a precision that has never been possible before. This will help identify the causes of disorders like [[grafo/termos/depression|depression]], [[grafo/termos/anxiety|anxiety]], schizophrenia, [[grafo/termos/addiction|addiction]], [[grafo/termos/sleep disorders|sleep disorder]], and autism. Optogenetics could improve upon existing implanted devices that are used to treat Parkinson’s disease, [[grafo/termos/obsessive-compulsive disorder|obsessive-compulsive disorder]] and other ailments with pulses of electricity. An optogenetics device could hit more specific subsets of brain cells than those devices can. Applications of optogenetic tools in nonneuronal cells are on the rise.
