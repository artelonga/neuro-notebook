The [[grafo/termos/ventral tegmental area|ventral tegmental area]] (VTA) and [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars compacta|substantia nigra pars compacta]] (SNc) of the midbrain are associated with Parkinson's disease (PD), schizophrenia, [[grafo/termos/mood disorders|mood disorders]] and [[grafo/termos/addiction|addiction]]. Based on the recently unraveled heterogeneity within the VTA and SNc, where [[grafo/termos/glutamate|glutamate]], [[grafo/termos/gaba|GABA]] and co-releasing neurons have been found to co-exist with the classical [[grafo/termos/dopamine neurons|dopamine neurons]], there is a compelling need for identification of [[grafo/termos/gene expression|gene expression]] patterns that represent this heterogeneity and that are of value for development of human therapies. Here, several unique gene expression patterns were identified in the mouse midbrain of which NeuroD6 and Grp were expressed within different dopaminergic subpopulations of the VTA, and [[grafo/termos/trpv1|TrpV1]] within a small heterogeneous population. Optogenetics-coupled in vivo amperometry revealed a previously unknown glutamatergic mesoaccumbal pathway characterized by TrpV1-Cre-expression. Human GRP was strongly detected in non-melanized [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]] within the SNc of both control and PD brains, suggesting GRP as a marker for neuroprotected neurons in PD. This study thus unravels markers for distinct subpopulations of neurons within the mouse and human midbrain, defines unique anatomical subregions within the VTA and exposes an entirely new glutamatergic pathway. Finally, both TRPV1 and GRP are implied in midbrain physiology of importance to neurological and [[grafo/termos/neuropsychiatric disorders|neuropsychiatric disorders]].
