[[grafo/termos/dopamine|Dopamine]] is thought to play a critical role in reinforcement learning and goal-directed behavior, but its function in [[grafo/termos/action selection|action selection]] remains largely unknown. Here we demonstrate that [[grafo/termos/nigrostriatal dopamine|nigrostriatal dopamine]] biases ongoing action selection. When mice were trained to dynamically switch the action selected at different time points, changes in [[grafo/termos/firing rate|firing rate]] of nigrostriatal dopamine neurons, as well as [[grafo/termos/dopamine signaling|dopamine signaling]] in the [[grafo/termos/dorsal striatum|dorsal striatum]], were found to be associated with action selection. This dopamine profile is specific to behavioral choice, scalable with interval duration, and doesn't reflect [[grafo/termos/reward|reward]] prediction error, timing, or value as single factors alone. Genetic deletion of [[grafo/termos/nmda receptor|NMDA receptors]] on dopamine or [[grafo/termos/striatal neurons|striatal neurons]] or optogenetic manipulation of dopamine concentration alters dopamine signaling and biases action selection. These results unveil a crucial role of nigrostriatal dopamine in integrating diverse information for regulating upcoming actions, and they have important implications for [[grafo/termos/neurological disorders|neurological disorders]], including Parkinson's disease and substance dependence.
