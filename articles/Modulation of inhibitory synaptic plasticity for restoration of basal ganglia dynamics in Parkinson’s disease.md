Introduction Parkinson’s disease is characterized, in part, by hypoactivity of both [[grafo/termos/direct pathway|direct pathway]] inhibitory projections from [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]] to the [[grafo/termos/globus pallidus internus|globus pallidus internus]] (GPi) and [[grafo/termos/indirect pathway|indirect pathway]] inhibitory projections from [[grafo/termos/globus pallidus externus|globus pallidus externus]] ([[grafo/termos/external globus pallidus|GPe]]) to the [[grafo/termos/subthalamic nucleus|subthalamic nucleus]] (STN), giving rise to disrupted [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia circuit|basal ganglia circuit]] activity. In this study, we explored the use of intracranial stimulation for eliciting [[grafo/termos/long-term potentiation|long-term potentiation]] (LTP) of each of these pathologically underactive inhibitory projections for the restoration of basal ganglia circuit dynamics and amelioration of [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptoms]]. Methods Data were collected from a total of 31 people with Parkinson’s disease (42 hemispheres). During [[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|deep brain stimulation]] (DBS) surgery, we assessed microelectrode stimulation-induced changes to inhibitory evoked field potentials (fEP) and hand kinematics before versus after a 40-second train of high-frequency stimulation (HFS) in the GPi (n = 7, 11 sites at 100 Hz) and STN (n = 10, 14 sites at 100 Hz; n = 4, 7 sites at 180 Hz). Additionally, we assessed changes to [[grafo/termos/beta oscillations|beta oscillations]] and hand kinematics in people with chronic DBS implants in the GPi (n = 6 at 125 Hz) and STN (n = 4 at 180 Hz). Results Intraoperatively, increases in fEP amplitude (p = 0.002) and improved motor performance (p = 0.003) were observed after 100 Hz HFS in the GPi; while in STN, HFS did not potentiate fEPs (p = 0.589) or improve motor performance (p = 0.460) (similar results yielded for 180 Hz in STN). Similarly, extraoperative GPi-DBS produced suppression of beta power (p=0.096) and motor improvement (p = 0.077) before versus after HFS at 125 Hz; while STN-DBS at 180 Hz did not significantly affect beta power (p = 0.267) or motor performance (p=0.850). Interpretation Our findings support that LTP-like effects in GPi may produce motor improvements that extend beyond stimulation cessation, aligning with optogenetic studies showing long-lasting [[grafo/termos/motor recovery|motor recovery]] through periodic D1-striatal activation in rodents. The lack of effects in STN suggests that stimulation paradigms may require optimization for effective LTP induction. These findings nevertheless highlight the potential of LTP-based strategies for sustained [[grafo/termos/therapeutic benefits|therapeutic benefits]] in Parkinson’s disease, warranting further investigation into periodic stimulation paradigms for optimizing DBS efficacy and side effect profiles.
