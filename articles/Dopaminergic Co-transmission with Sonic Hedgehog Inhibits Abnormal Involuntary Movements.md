[[grafo/termos/l-dopa-induced dyskinesia|L-Dopa induced dyskinesia]] ([[grafo/termos/levodopa-induced dyskinesia|LID]]) is a debilitating side effect of [[grafo/termos/dopamine replacement therapy|dopamine replacement therapy]] for Parkinson’s Disease. The mechanistic underpinnings of LID remain obscure. Here we report that diminshed sonic hedgehog (Shh) signaling in the [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia|basal ganglia]] caused by the degeneration of [[grafo/termos/midbrain dopamine neurons|midbrain dopamine neurons]] (DANs) facilitates the formation and expression of LID. We demonstrate that augmenting Shh signaling with agonists of the Shh effector Smoothened attenuates LID in mouse and macaque models of PD. Employing conditional genetic loss-of-function approaches, we show that reducing Shh secretion from DANs or Smo activity in [[grafo/termos/cholinergic interneurons|cholinergic interneurons]] (CINs) promotes LID. Conversely, the selective expression of constitutively active Smo (SmoM2) in CINs is sufficient to render the sensitized aphakia model of PD resistant to LID. Furthermore, acute depletion of Shh from DANs through prolonged optogenetic stimulation in otherwise intact mice and in the absence of [[grafo/termos/l-dopa|L-Dopa]] produces LID-like [[grafo/termos/involuntary movements|involuntary movements]]. These findings indicate that augmenting Shh signaling in the L-Dopa treated brain may be a promising and unexpected novel therapeutic approach for mitigating the dyskinetic side effects of long-term treatment with L-Dopa
