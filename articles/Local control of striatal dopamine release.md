The mesolimbic and [[grafo/termos/nigrostriatal dopamine|nigrostriatal dopamine]] (DA) systems play a key role in the physiology of [[grafo/termos/reward seeking|reward seeking]], [[grafo/termos/motivation|motivation]] and [[grafo/termos/motor control|motor control]]. Importantly, they are also involved in the pathophysiology of Parkinson's and Huntington's disease, schizophrenia and [[grafo/termos/addiction|addiction]]. Control of DA release in the [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]] is tightly linked to firing of DA neurons in the [[grafo/termos/ventral tegmental area|ventral tegmental area]] (VTA) and the [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra|substantia nigra]] (SN). However, local influences in the striatum affect release by exerting their action directly on [[grafo/termos/axon terminals|axon terminals]]. For example, endogenous glutamatergic and cholinergic activity is sufficient to trigger striatal DA release independently of cell body firing. Recent developments involving genetic manipulation, pharmacological selectivity or selective stimulation have allowed for better characterization of these phenomena. Such termino-terminal forms of control of DA release transform considerably our understanding of the mesolimbic and nigrostriatal systems, and have strong implications as potential mechanisms to modify impaired control of DA release in the diseased brain. Here, we review these and related mechanisms and their implications in the physiology of ascending DA systems.
