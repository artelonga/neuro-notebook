The [[grafo/termos/entorhinal cortex|entorhinal cortex]] (EC) is a critical [[grafo/termos/brain areas|brain area]] for [[grafo/termos/memory formation|memory formation]], while also the region exhibiting the earliest histological and [[grafo/termos/functional alterations|functional alterations]] in Alzheimer's disease (AD). The EC thus has been long hypothesized as one of the originating brain areas of AD pathophysiology, although [[grafo/termos/circuit mechanisms|circuit mechanisms]] causing its selective vulnerability remain [[grafo/termos/poorly understood|poorly understood]]. We found that [[grafo/termos/dopamine neurons|dopamine neurons]] projecting their axons to the lateral EC (LEC), critical for memory formation in healthy brains, become dysfunctional and cause [[grafo/termos/memory impairment|memory impairments]] in early AD brains. In [[grafo/termos/amyloid precursor protein|amyloid precursor protein]] knock-in mice with associative memory impairment, LEC [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] activity and associative [[grafo/termos/memory|memory]] encoding of LEC layer 2/3 neurons were disrupted in parallel from the early pathological stage. Optogenetic reactivation of LEC dopamine fibers, as well as L- DOPA treatment, rescued associative learning behavior. These results suggest that dysfunction of LEC-projecting dopamine neurons underlies memory impairment in AD from [[grafo/termos/early stages|early stages]], pointing to a need for clinical investigation of LEC dopamine in AD patients.
