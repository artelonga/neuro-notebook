Previous studies in human patients and [[grafo/termos/rodent models|rodent models]] of Parkinson’s disease (PD) have established [[grafo/termos/neuroprotection|neuroprotection]] of dopaminergic (DA) neurons in [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars compacta|substantia nigra pars compacta]] (SNC) by physical exercise, but the precise origin of this neuroprotective effect has yet to be elucidated. In this study, we tested a hypothesis that enhanced activity of DA neurons in SNC results in neuroprotection using the unilateral [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-hydroxydopamine]] (6-OHDA) injection model in mice. To increase activity of DA neurons chronically and specifically, we injected an adeno-associated [[grafo/termos/viral vectors|viral vector]] carrying a step-function luminopsin (SFL) – a fusion protein of light-emitting Gaussia luciferase and light-sensing step-function [[grafo/termos/channelrhodopsin-2|channelrhodopsin 2]] – into SNC ipsilateral to 6-OHDA using the pan-neuronal human synapsin I promoter or the Cre-lox system in transgenic mice expressing the recombinase under control of the [[grafo/termos/tyrosine hydroxylase|tyrosine hydroxylase]] (TH) promoter. Upon application of SFL substrate, coelenterazine (CTZ), the luciferase moiety of luminopsin emits bioluminescence which in turn activate the opsin moiety. Daily injection of CTZ for 4 weeks ameliorated a stereotypical behavior, namely ipsiversive rotations, induced by unilateral 6-OHDA. In addition, postmortem immunohistochemistry against TH revealed less severe [[grafo/termos/neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]] of DA neurons compared to vehicle-injected control animals. Furthermore, when mice were pretreated with ANA-12, a selective antagonist for tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB), the behavioral improvement and neuroprotective effect were diminished. These results suggest that increased [[grafo/termos/neuronal activity|neuronal activity]] of DA neurons provides neuroprotection against 6-OHDA injury and alleviates its symptoms through the brain-derived neurotrophic factor-TrkB pathway.
