ABSTRACT GABAergic projections neurons of the [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra|substantia nigra]] reticulata ([[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars reticulata|SNr]]), through an extensive network of dendritic arbors and axon collaterals, provide robust [[grafo/termos/inhibitory input|inhibitory input]] to neighboring [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]] in the substantia nigra compacta ([[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars compacta|SNc]]). Angiotensin-II (Ang-II) [[grafo/termos/receptor signaling|receptor signaling]] increases SNc dopaminergic neuronal sensitivity to insult, thus rendering these cells susceptible to dysfunction and destruction. However, the mechanisms by which Ang-II regulates SNc dopaminergic [[grafo/termos/neuronal activity|neuronal activity]] are unclear. Given the complex relationship between SN dopaminergic and [[grafo/termos/gabaergic neurons|GABAergic neurons]], we hypothesized that Ang-II could regulate SNc dopaminergic neuronal activity directly and indirectly by modulating SNr GABAergic neurotransmission. Herein, using transgenic mice, slice [[grafo/termos/electrophysiology|electrophysiology]], and optogenetics, we provide evidence of an AT 1 receptor-mediated signaling mechanism in SNr GABAergic neurons where Ang-II suppresses electrically-evoked neuronal output by facilitating postsynaptic [[grafo/termos/gaba|GABA]] A receptors and prolonging the [[grafo/termos/action potentials|action potential]] duration. Unexpectedly, Ang-II had no discernable effects on the electrical properties of SNc dopaminergic neurons. Also, and indicating a nonlinear relationship between electrical activity and neuronal output, following phasic photoactivation of SNr GABAergic neurons, Ang-II paradoxically enhanced the feedforward inhibitory input to SNc dopaminergic neurons. In sum, our observations describe an increasingly complex and heterogeneous response of the SN to Ang-II by revealing cell-specific responses and nonlinear effects on intranigral GABAergic neurotransmission. Our data further implicate the renin-angiotensin-system as a functionally relevant neuromodulator in the [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia|basal ganglia]], thus underscoring a need for additional inquiry. SIGNIFICANCE STATEMENT Angiotensin II (Ang-II) promotes [[grafo/termos/dopamine release|dopamine release]] in the [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]] and, in the substantia nigra compacta (SNc), exacerbates [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic cells|dopaminergic cell]] loss in animal models of Parkinson’s disease. Despite a potential association with Parkinson’s disease, the effects of Ang-II on neuronal activity in the basal ganglia is unknown. Here we describe a novel AT 1 receptor-dependent signaling mechanism in GABAergic [[grafo/termos/projection neurons|projection neurons]] of the SN reticulata (SNr), a major inhibitory regulator of SNc dopaminergic neurons. Specifically, Ang-II suppresses SNr GABAergic neuronal activity, subsequently altering GABAergic modulation of SNc dopaminergic neurons in a nonlinear fashion. Altogether, our data provide the first indication of Ang-II-dependent modulation of GABAergic neurotransmission in the SN, which could impact output from the basal ganglia in health and disease.
