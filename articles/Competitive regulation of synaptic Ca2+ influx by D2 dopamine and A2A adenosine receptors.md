Striatal D2-type [[grafo/termos/dopamine receptors|dopamine receptors]] (D2Rs) have been implicated in the pathophysiology of [[grafo/termos/neuropsychiatric disorders|neuropsychiatric disorders]], including Parkinson's disease and schizophrenia. Although these receptors regulate striatal [[grafo/termos/synaptic plasticity|synaptic plasticity]], the [[grafo/termos/mechanisms underlying|mechanisms underlying]] dopaminergic modulation of [[grafo/termos/glutamatergic synapses|glutamatergic synapses]] are unclear. We combined optogenetics, [[grafo/termos/two-photon microscopy|two-photon microscopy]] and [[grafo/termos/glutamate|glutamate]] uncaging to examine D2R-dependent modulation of glutamatergic [[grafo/termos/synaptic transmission|synaptic transmission]] in mouse [[grafo/termos/striatopallidal neurons|striatopallidal neurons]]. We found that D2R activation reduces corticostriatal glutamate release and attenuates both synaptic- and action potential-evoked Ca2+ influx into [[grafo/termos/dendritic spine|dendritic spines]] by approximately 50%. Modulation of Ca2+ signaling was mediated by a [[grafo/termos/protein kinase|protein kinase]] A (PKA)-dependent regulation of Ca2+ entry through NMDA-type [[grafo/termos/glutamate receptors|glutamate receptors]] that was inhibited by D2Rs and enhanced by activation of 2A-type adenosine receptors (A2ARs). D2Rs also produced a PKA- and A2AR-independent reduction in Ca2+ influx through R-type voltage-gated Ca2+ channels. These findings reveal that [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] regulates spine Ca2+ by multiple pathways and that competitive modulation of PKA controls NMDAR-mediated Ca2+ signaling in the [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]].
