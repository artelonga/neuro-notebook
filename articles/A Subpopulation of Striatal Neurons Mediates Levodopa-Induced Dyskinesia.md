Parkinson's disease is characterized by the progressive loss of [[grafo/termos/midbrain dopamine neurons|midbrain dopamine neurons]]. [[grafo/termos/dopamine replacement therapy|Dopamine replacement therapy]] with [[grafo/termos/levodopa|levodopa]] [[grafo/termos/alleviates parkinsonian|alleviates parkinsonian]] [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptoms]] but is complicated by the development of [[grafo/termos/involuntary movements|involuntary movements]], termed [[grafo/termos/levodopa-induced dyskinesia|levodopa-induced dyskinesia]] (LID). Aberrant activity in the [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]] has been hypothesized to cause LID. Here, to establish a direct link between striatal activity and [[grafo/termos/dyskinesia|dyskinesia]], we combine optogenetics and a method to manipulate dyskinesia-associated neurons, targeted recombination in active populations (TRAP). We find that TRAPed cells are a stable subset of sensorimotor [[grafo/termos/striatal neurons|striatal neurons]], predominantly from the [[grafo/termos/direct pathway|direct pathway]], and that reactivation of TRAPed striatal neurons causes dyskinesia in the absence of levodopa. Inhibition of TRAPed cells, but not a nonspecific subset of direct pathway neurons, ameliorates LID. These results establish that a distinct subset of striatal neurons is causally involved in LID and indicate that successful [[grafo/termos/therapeutic strategies|therapeutic strategies]] for treating LID may require targeting functionally selective neuronal subtypes.
