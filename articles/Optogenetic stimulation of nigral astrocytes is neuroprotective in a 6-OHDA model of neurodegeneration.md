Summary Highlights Optogenetic stimulation of nigral [[grafo/termos/astrocytes|astrocytes]] attenuates [[grafo/termos/motor deficits|motor deficits]] & Th+ cell loss in a [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-OHDA]] model of [[grafo/termos/neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]] Bulk RNA-seq analysis reveals optogenetic stimulation of nigral astrocytes induces early changes in [[grafo/termos/microglia|microglia]] snRNA-seq shows 6-OHDA alone induces extensive [[grafo/termos/gene expression|gene expression]] changes across all [[grafo/termos/cell populations|cell populations]] within the SN Oligodendrocytes within the [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars compacta|SNc]] express Th, which is upregulated with DA neuron loss Parkinson’s disease is characterized by the loss of [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]] in the [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra|substantia nigra]]. Glial-glial crosstalk is essential for maintaining the regional milieu, and appears to be particularly important in modulating [[grafo/termos/neuroinflammation|neuroinflammation]] and many aspects of neurodegeneration. In particular, astrocytes are critical for maintaining [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] neuronal integrity and survival, and astroglial dysfunction is prominent in Parkinson’s disease. As such, astrocytes represent a potentially critical [[grafo/termos/therapeutic target|therapeutic target]] in neurodegeneration. In this study, in vivo optogenetics were used to selectively stimulate astrocytes in the substantia nigra following a striatal 6-OHDA lesion. Remarkably, a single bout of optogenetic stimulation was sufficient to attenuate motor deficits and [[grafo/termos/dopamine neurons|dopamine neuron]] loss induced by the neurotoxin. Furthermore, bulk RNA-seq and snRNA-seq analysis of the substantia nigra revealed extensive changes in both microglia and oligodendrocytes, suggesting that the neuroprotective effects of stimulating astrocytes may be mediated through alterations in glia-glia crosstalk. Altogether, this work demonstrates the importance of understanding glia-glia interactions in neurodegeneration.
