Abstract Working memory impairment is a core cognitive deficit in Alzheimer’s disease (AD), marked by a pronounced inability to retrieve stored information. Although dysfunction in the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC)–mediodorsal thalamus (MD) circuit has been implicated in this impairment, the precise cellular mechanisms and their potential as targets for intervention remain poorly understood. Here, using in vivo electrophysiological recordings in APP/PS1 mice during a spatial working memory task, we identified disrupted theta-frequency synchronization and reduced top-down information flow within the mPFC–MD circuit. At the cellular level, mPFC pyramidal neurons displayed diminished task-related activation and impaired spatial coding. Optogenetic activation of prefrontal pyramidal neurons normalized spatial encoding and spike-time locking, and rescued prefrontal-thalamic circuit function. Logistic regression analysis further identified mPFC pyramidal neuron firing rate as the key feature distinguishing optogenetic stimulation from sham stimulation, suggesting that restoring excitability of these neurons is an effective strategy for ameliorating AD working memory deficits. Our results identify prefrontal pyramidal neuron dysregulation as a critical driver of working memory deficits in AD and pinpoint specific, targetable neural activity signatures for cognitive restoration.
