OBJECTIVE: To establish optogenetic control system of phasic [[grafo/termos/dopamine release|dopamine release]] in the [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]] of mice in vivo. BACKGROUND: [[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|Deep brain stimulation]] (DBS) is a [[grafo/termos/therapeutic option|therapeutic option]] for medication refractory symptoms of [[grafo/termos/movement disorders|movement disorders]], including Parkinson’s disease. Traditional [[grafo/termos/electrical stimulation|electrical stimulation]], however, spreads surrounding structures, and may excite or inhibit all affected neurons and axons. Optogenetics is a new technology to control [[grafo/termos/cell activity|cell activity]] by combining genetic and optics. By introducing light-gated [[grafo/termos/ion channels|ion channels]] in the target neuron, turning [[grafo/termos/specific neurons|specific neurons]] on or off rapidly in response to diverse colors of light is capable. Utilizing optogentics may contribute to neuron-specific and less side-effect stimulation for [[grafo/termos/neuromodulation|neuromodulation]]. DESIGN/METHODS: AAV-EF1a-hChR-eYFP was injected into the [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra|substantia nigra]] (SN) of THcre mouse. An [[grafo/termos/optical fibers|optical fiber]] was positioned at the SN to deliver light. A carbon fiber electrode was implanted in the striatum and an Ag/AgCl reference electrode was positioned in the contralateral hemisphere. Optic stimulation by LED was delivered (470nm, 2ms, 60Hzx24 pulses) and striatal dopaminergic response was measured by using Fastscan cyclic [[grafo/termos/voltammetry|voltammetry]] (FSCV). After the recording of response of [[grafo/termos/optical stimulation|optical stimulation]], a pair of bipolar stimulating electrodes was implanted into the ipsilateral medial forebrain bundle (MFB) and electrical stimulation was applied (30Hz x24 pulses). RESULTS: Optical stimulation of MFB [[grafo/termos/dopamine neurons|dopamine neurons]] induced striatal dopaminergic release detected by FSCV, as well as electrical stimulation did. Dopaminergic release was induced by optical stimulation depended on pulse width (1-5ms), frequency (15-60Hz), Light power (0.4-1.4mW), and depth of electrode (4.375-4.825mm). CONCLUSIONS: We demonstrated that optical stimulation induced striatal dopaminergic release, as well as electrical stimulation did. Our data suggest that optogenetics can be applied to neuromodulation instead of traditional electrical stimulation although there are many unknown parameter for better control of [[grafo/termos/neuronal circuits|neuronal circuit]] using this new technology.
