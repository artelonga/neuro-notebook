The [[grafo/termos/small molecule|small molecule]] protein [[grafo/termos/α-synuclein|α-synuclein]] forms insoluble aggregates in a group of [[grafo/termos/neurological disorders|neurological disorders]], including Parkinson’s disease and dementia with [[grafo/termos/lewy bodies|Lewy bodies]] (DLB), which are collectively called synucleinopathies. In PD and DLB, the amygdala has been identified as a particularly susceptible region in the brain for the deposition of Lewy-like α-synuclein aggregates. Though α-synuclein aggregation is closely associated with [[grafo/termos/neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]], there is a poor correlation between neurodegeneration in the amygdala and the clinical features of PD/DLB. We hypothesize that, prior to neurodegeneration, α-synuclein aggregation disrupts functional cortical modulation of the amygdala circuits, leading to emotion dysregulation in synucleinopathies. In the present study, we combined [[grafo/termos/electrophysiology|electrophysiology]], optogenetics, mouse model of synucleinopathies, and behavioral analysis to test this hypothesis. Using an α-synuclein preformed fibrils (PFFs)-based mouse model of synucleinopathies, we reported dynamic changes in the levels of α-synuclein pathology in the [[grafo/termos/basolateral amygdala|basolateral amygdala]] (BLA). Such dynamic changes of pathology associated with a decreased cortico-BLA connection strength prior to a significant loss of cortical [[grafo/termos/axon terminals|axon terminals]]. In parallel to the reduced cortico-BLA connection, PFFs-injected mice manifested impaired social preference behavior. The impaired sociability of PFFs-injected mice could be rescued by [[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|chemogenetic]] stimulation of cortico-BLA inputs. Altogether, we presented a series of evidence to delineate key circuit events associated with α-synuclein pathology development in the amygdala circuits. The present work highlights the necessity of a thorough investigation of functional consequences of α-synuclein aggregation to advance our understand of pathophysiology of synucleinopathies and development of effective therapies.
