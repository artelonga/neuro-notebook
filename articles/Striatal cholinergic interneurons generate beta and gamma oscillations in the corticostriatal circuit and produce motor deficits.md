Cortico-basal ganglia-thalamic (CBT) [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuits]] are critical modulators of cognitive and [[grafo/termos/motor function|motor function]]. When compromised, these circuits contribute to neurological and [[grafo/termos/psychiatric disorders|psychiatric disorders]], such as Parkinson's disease (PD). In PD, [[grafo/termos/motor deficits|motor deficits]] correlate with the emergence of exaggerated [[grafo/termos/beta frequency|beta frequency]] (15-30 Hz) oscillations throughout the CBT network. However, little is known about how [[grafo/termos/specific cell types|specific cell types]] within individual CBT [[grafo/termos/brain regions|brain regions]] support the generation, propagation, and interaction of oscillatory dynamics throughout the CBT circuit or how specific oscillatory dynamics are related to motor function. Here, we investigated the role of [[grafo/termos/striatal cholinergic interneurons|striatal cholinergic interneurons]] (SChIs) in generating beta and [[grafo/termos/gamma oscillations|gamma oscillations]] in cortical-striatal circuits and in influencing movement behavior. We found that selective stimulation of SChIs via optogenetics in normal mice robustly and reversibly amplified beta and gamma oscillations that are supported by distinct mechanisms within striatal-cortical circuits. Whereas [[grafo/termos/beta oscillations|beta oscillations]] are supported robustly in the [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]] and all layers of [[grafo/termos/primary motor cortex|primary motor cortex]] (M1) through a [[grafo/termos/muscarinic receptors|muscarinic-receptor]] mediated mechanism, gamma oscillations are largely restricted to the striatum and the deeper layers of M1. Finally, SChI activation led to parkinsonian-like motor deficits in otherwise normal mice. These results highlight the important role of striatal cholinergic interneurons in supporting oscillations in the CBT network that are closely related to movement and [[grafo/termos/parkinsonian motor symptoms|parkinsonian motor symptoms]].
