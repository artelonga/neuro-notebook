The pattern of activity of [[grafo/termos/globus pallidus|globus pallidus]] (GP) neurons is tightly regulated by [[grafo/termos/gabaergic inhibition|GABAergic inhibition]]. In addition to extrinsic inputs from the [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]] (STR-GP) the other source of [[grafo/termos/gaba|GABA]] to GP neurons arises from intrinsic intranuclear axon collaterals (GP-GP). While the contribution of striatal inputs has been studied, notably its hyperactivity in Parkinson's disease (PD), the properties and function of intranuclear inhibition remain [[grafo/termos/poorly understood|poorly understood]]. Our objective was therefore to test the impact of chronic [[grafo/termos/dopamine depletion|dopamine depletion]] on pallido-pallidal transmission. Using patch-clamp whole-cell recordings in rat [[grafo/termos/brain slices|brain slices]], we combined electrical and optogenetic stimulations with pharmacology to differentiate basic [[grafo/termos/synaptic properties|synaptic properties]] of STR-GP and GP-GP GABAergic [[grafo/termos/synapse|synapses]]. GP-GP synapses were characterized by activity-dependent [[grafo/termos/depression|depression]] and insensitivity to the D(2) receptor specific agonist quinpirole and STR-GP synapses by frequency-dependent facilitation and quinpirole modulation. Chronic [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] deprivation obtained in [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-OHDA]] lesioned animals boosted the amplitude of GP-GP IPSCs but did not modify STR-GP transmission and increased the amplitude of miniature IPSCs. Replacement of calcium by strontium confirmed that the quantal amplitude was increased at GP-GP synapses. Finally, we demonstrated that boosted GP-GP transmission promotes resetting of autonomous activity and rebound-burst firing after dopamine depletion. These results suggest that GP-GP [[grafo/termos/synaptic transmission|synaptic transmission]] (but not STR-GP) is augmented by chronic dopamine depletion which could contribute to the aberrant GP [[grafo/termos/neuronal activity|neuronal activity]] observed in PD.
