Short-term declarative [[grafo/termos/memory impairment|memory impairment]], encompassing deficits in [[grafo/termos/spatial working memory|spatial working memory]] and object recognition, represents a prevalent [[grafo/termos/non-motor symptoms|non-motor symptom]] of Parkinson's disease (PD). Despite its clinical significance, the [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuit]] [[grafo/termos/mechanisms underlying|mechanisms underlying]] this [[grafo/termos/cognitive dysfunction|cognitive dysfunction]] remain [[grafo/termos/poorly understood|poorly understood]]. Here, we identify a previously underappreciated dopaminergic projection from the [[grafo/termos/locus coeruleus|locus coeruleus]] (LC) to the [[grafo/termos/retrosplenial cortex|retrosplenial cortex]] (RSC) as a critical regulator of short-term declarative [[grafo/termos/memory|memory]]. Using optogenetic activation of LC [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]], we evoked [[grafo/termos/dopamine release|dopamine release]] in the RSC and combined this manipulation with [[grafo/termos/fiber photometry|fiber photometry]] to monitor calcium dynamics during short-term declarative memory tasks, including the Y-maze and novel object recognition (NOR). These experiments revealed task-dependent activation of the LC-RSC pathway. Notably, this pathway exhibited [[grafo/termos/functional impairments|functional impairment]] in PD model mice, as LC-evoked dopaminergic signals in the RSC were markedly attenuated and accompanied by reduced [[grafo/termos/tyrosine hydroxylase|tyrosine hydroxylase]] expression in the LC. To establish causality, we selectively inhibited the LC-RSC pathway using a [[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|chemogenetic]] approach, which recapitulated deficits in spatial working memory and object recognition and disrupted [[grafo/termos/local field potentials|local field potential]] (LFP) activity in the RSC, demonstrating that this pathway is necessary for normal short-term declarative memory processing. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the LC-RSC dopaminergic pathway plays a pivotal role in mediating PD-related short-term declarative memory impairment, providing a circuit-level framework and potential [[grafo/termos/therapeutic target|therapeutic target]] for early cognitive intervention in PD.
