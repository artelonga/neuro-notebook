Alzheimer's disease (AD) is the most [[grafo/termos/common neurodegenerative|common neurodegenerative]] disease and has an insidious onset. Exploring the characteristics and mechanism of the early symptoms of AD plays a critical role in the early diagnosis and intervention of AD. Here we found that depressive-like behavior and short-term [[grafo/termos/spatial memory|spatial memory]] dysfunction appeared in APPswe/PS1dE9 mice (AD mice) as early as 9-11 weeks of age. Electrophysiological analysis revealed excitatory/inhibitory (E/I) imbalance in the [[grafo/termos/prefrontal cortex|prefrontal cortex]] (PFC). This E/I imbalance was induced by significant reduction in the number and activity of [[grafo/termos/parvalbumin interneurons|parvalbumin interneurons]] (PV+ INs) in this region. Furthermore, optogenetic and [[grafo/termos/chemogenetic activation|chemogenetic activation]] of residual PV+ INs effectively ameliorated depressive-like behavior and rescued short-term spatial memory in AD mice. These results suggest the PFC is selectively vulnerable in the [[grafo/termos/early stages|early stage]] of AD and prefrontal PV+ INs deficits play a key role in the occurrence and development of early symptoms of AD.
