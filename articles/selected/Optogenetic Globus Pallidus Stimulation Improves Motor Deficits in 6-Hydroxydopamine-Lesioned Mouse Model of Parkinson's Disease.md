Excessive inhibition of the [[grafo/termos/external globus pallidus|external globus pallidus]] (GPe) by striatal [[grafo/termos/gabaergic neurons|GABAergic neurons]] is considered a central mechanism contributing to [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptoms]] of Parkinson's disease (PD). While electrophysiological findings support this view, behavioral studies assessing the beneficial effects of global GPe activations are scarce and the reported results are controversial. We used an optogenetic approach and the standard unilateral [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-hydroxydopamine]] [[grafo/termos/nigrostriatal dopamine|nigrostriatal dopamine]] (DA) lesion model of PD to explore the effects of GPe photostimulation on [[grafo/termos/motor deficits|motor deficits]] in mice. Global optogenetic GPe inhibition was used in normal mice to verify whether it reproduced the typical [[grafo/termos/motor impairment|motor impairment]] induced by DA lesions. GPe activation improved ipsilateral circling, contralateral forelimb [[grafo/termos/akinesia|akinesia]], locomotor hypoactivity, and [[grafo/termos/bradykinesia|bradykinesia]] in 6-OHDA-lesioned mice at ineffective photostimulation parameters (532 nm, 5 Hz, 3 mW) in normal mice. GPe photoinhibition (450 nm, 12 mW) had no effect on [[grafo/termos/locomotor activity|locomotor activity]] and forelimb use in normal mice. Bilateral photoinhibition (450 nm, 6 mW/side) reduced directed exploration and improved [[grafo/termos/working memory|working memory]] performances indicating that recruitment of GPe in physiological conditions may depend on the behavioral task involved. Collectively, these findings shed new light on the functional role of GPe and suggest that it is a promising target for neuromodulatory restoration of motor deficits in PD.
