[[grafo/termos/high-frequency deep brain|High-frequency deep brain]] stimulation ([[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|DBS]]) at [[grafo/termos/subthalamic nucleus|subthalamic nucleus]] (STN) is an effective therapy for Parkinson's disease (PD), but the [[grafo/termos/underlying mechanisms|underlying mechanisms]] remain unclear. Here we find an important role of asynchronous release (AR) of [[grafo/termos/gaba|GABA]] induced by high-frequency stimulation (HFS) in alleviating [[grafo/termos/motor function|motor functions]] of dopamine-depleted male mice. [[grafo/termos/electrophysiological recordings|Electrophysiological recordings]] reveal that 130-Hz HFS causes an initial inhibition followed by desynchronization of STN neurons, largely attributable to presynaptic [[grafo/termos/gaba release|GABA release]]. Low-frequency stimulation at 20 Hz, however, produces much weaker AR and negligible effects on [[grafo/termos/neuronal firing|neuronal firing]]. Further optogenetic and cell-ablation experiments demonstrate that activation of [[grafo/termos/parvalbumin|parvalbumin]] axons, but not non-parvalbumin axons, from [[grafo/termos/external globus pallidus|external globus pallidus]] (GPe) is both necessary and sufficient for DBS effects. Reducing AR diminishes the high-frequency DBS effect, while increasing AR allows low-frequency DBS to achieve a [[grafo/termos/therapeutic benefits|therapeutic benefit]]. Therefore, asynchronous GABA release from GPe PV neurons may contribute significantly to the therapeutic effects of high-frequency DBS.
