The [[grafo/termos/pedunculopontine nucleus|pedunculopontine nucleus]] (PPN) is a locomotor command area containing [[grafo/termos/glutamatergic neurons|glutamatergic neurons]] that control locomotor initiation and maintenance. These motor actions are deficient in Parkinson's disease (PD), where dopaminergic [[grafo/termos/neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]] alters [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia|basal ganglia]] activity. Being downstream of the basal ganglia, the PPN may be a suitable target for [[grafo/termos/ameliorating parkinsonian motor|ameliorating parkinsonian motor]] symptoms. Here, we use in vivo [[grafo/termos/cell-type specific|cell-type specific]] PPN activation to restore [[grafo/termos/motor function|motor function]] in two mouse models of [[grafo/termos/parkinsonism|parkinsonism]] made by acute pharmacological blockage of [[grafo/termos/dopamine transmission|dopamine transmission]]. With a combination of chemo- and opto-genetics, we show that excitation of caudal glutamatergic PPN neurons can normalize the otherwise severe [[grafo/termos/locomotor deficits|locomotor deficit]] in PD, whereas targeting the local GABAergic population only leads to recovery of slow [[grafo/termos/locomotion|locomotion]]. The motor rescue driven by glutamatergic PPN activation is independent of activity in nearby locomotor promoting glutamatergic Cuneiform neurons. Our observations point to caudal glutamatergic PPN neurons as a potential target for neuromodulatory restoration of locomotor function in PD.
