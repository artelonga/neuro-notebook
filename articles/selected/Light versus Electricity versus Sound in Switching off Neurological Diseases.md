Neurological diseases, ranging from Parkinson’s and [[grafo/termos/epilepsy|epilepsy]] to [[grafo/termos/chronic pain|chronic pain]] and [[grafo/termos/depression|depression]], remain a major medical challenge, often resistant to conventional [[grafo/termos/pharmacological treatments|pharmacological treatments]]. Emerging evidence suggests that alternative modalities such as light, electricity, and sound can modulate [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuits]], offering promising strategies to “switch off” pathological activity. Phototherapy and optogenetics allow targeted [[grafo/termos/neuronal activation|neuronal activation]] or inhibition using [[grafo/termos/light-sensitive proteins|light-sensitive proteins]], while [[grafo/termos/electrical stimulation|electrical stimulation]]—including [[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|deep brain stimulation]] and transcranial direct current stimulation—modulates neural excitability and [[grafo/termos/network dynamics|network dynamics]]. Similarly, sound-based approaches, including [[grafo/termos/focused ultrasound|focused ultrasound]] and vibrotactile [[grafo/termos/neuromodulation|neuromodulation]], influence [[grafo/termos/brain activity|brain activity]] non-invasively. This article examines the comparative potential of these modalities, highlighting their mechanisms, advantages, and limitations in managing [[grafo/termos/neurological disorders|neurological disorders]]. While each technique shows promise, their optimal application may involve complementary strategies tailored to disease-specific circuits. Advancing these interventions requires rigorous research, ethical considerations, and translational studies to harness the full potential of light, electricity, and sound in restoring neurological health.
