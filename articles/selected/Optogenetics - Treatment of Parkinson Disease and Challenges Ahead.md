Optogenetic tools such as photosensitive opsins and LEDs are used for attaining spatial and temporal precision in neurons to study many [[grafo/termos/neurodegenerative disorders|neurodegenerative disorders]], particularly Parkinson Disease (PD). This novel light-based technology has been reported in studying [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia|basal ganglia]] to understand the [[grafo/termos/direct and indirect pathways|direct and indirect pathways]]. The role of optogenetics in unveiling the [[grafo/termos/molecular basis|molecular basis]] of PD: [[grafo/termos/α-synuclein|α-synuclein]] aggregation and its association with the loss of [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]] is also highlighted. The very frequently used [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-OHDA]] model has also been manipulated optogenetically to study the progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons. [[grafo/termos/optical stimulation|Optical stimulation]] of neurons at high frequencies has been observed to be helpful in the amelioration of PD [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptoms]] such as [[grafo/termos/bradykinesia|Bradykinesia]] and Hypokinesia. PD treatment methods such as [[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|Deep Brain stimulation]] and [[grafo/termos/levodopa|Levodopa]] treatment have also been optogenetically modulated by many researchers to understand the [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia circuitry|basal ganglia circuitry]] revealing the inner mechanism of PD.
