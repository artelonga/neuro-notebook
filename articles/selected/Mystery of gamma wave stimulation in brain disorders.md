[[grafo/termos/neuronal oscillations|Neuronal oscillations]] refer to rhythmic and periodic fluctuations of electrical activity in the [[grafo/termos/central nervous|central nervous]] system that arise from the cellular properties of diverse [[grafo/termos/neuronal populations|neuronal populations]] and their interactions. Specifically, [[grafo/termos/gamma oscillations|gamma oscillations]] play a crucial role in governing the connectivity between distinct [[grafo/termos/brain regions|brain regions]], which are essential in perception, [[grafo/termos/motor control|motor control]], [[grafo/termos/memory|memory]], and emotions. In this context, we recapitulate various current stimulation methods to induce gamma entrainment. These methods include sensory stimulation, optogenetic modulation, photobiomodulation, and transcranial electrical or [[grafo/termos/magnetic stimulation|magnetic stimulation]]. Simultaneously, we explore the association between abnormal gamma oscillations and central nervous system disorders such as Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, [[grafo/termos/stroke|stroke]], schizophrenia, and [[grafo/termos/autism spectrum disorder|autism spectrum disorders]]. Evidence suggests that gamma entrainment-inducing stimulation methods offer notable [[grafo/termos/neuroprotection|neuroprotection]], although somewhat controversial. This review comprehensively discusses the functional role of gamma oscillations in higher-order brain activities from both physiological and pathological perspectives, emphasizing gamma entrainment as a potential therapeutic approach for [[grafo/termos/neuropsychiatric disorders|neuropsychiatric disorders]]. Additionally, we discuss future opportunities and challenges in implementing such strategies.
