Current [[grafo/termos/pharmacological treatments|pharmacological treatments]] for Parkinson's disease (PD) are focused on symptomatic relief, but not on disease modification, based on the strong belief that PD is caused by irreversible dopaminergic [[grafo/termos/neuronal death|neuronal death]]. Thus, the concept of the presence of dormant [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]] and its possibility as the disease-modifying [[grafo/termos/therapeutic target|therapeutic target]] against PD have not been explored. Here we show that optogenetic activation of [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars compacta|substantia nigra pars compacta]] (SNpc) neurons alleviates [[grafo/termos/parkinsonism|parkinsonism]] in acute PD animal models by recovering [[grafo/termos/tyrosine hydroxylase|tyrosine hydroxylase]] (TH) from the TH-negative dormant dopaminergic neurons, some of which still express DOPA decarboxylase (DDC). The TH loss depends on reduced dopaminergic [[grafo/termos/neuronal firing|neuronal firing]] under aberrant tonic inhibition, which is attributed to excessive astrocytic [[grafo/termos/gaba|GABA]]. Blocking the astrocytic GABA synthesis recapitulates the therapeutic effect of optogenetic activation. Consistently, SNpc of postmortem PD patients shows a significant population of TH-negative/DDC-positive dormant neurons surrounded by numerous GABA-positive [[grafo/termos/astrocytes|astrocytes]]. We propose that disinhibiting dormant dopaminergic neurons by blocking excessive astrocytic GABA could be an [[grafo/termos/effective therapeutic|effective therapeutic]] strategy against PD.
