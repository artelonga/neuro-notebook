Neurogenesis is a key mechanism of brain development and plasticity, which is impaired in chronic [[grafo/termos/neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]], including Parkinson's disease. The accumulation of aberrant [[grafo/termos/α-synuclein|α-synuclein]] is one of the features of PD. Being secreted, this protein produces a prominent neurotoxic effect, alters [[grafo/termos/synaptic plasticity|synaptic plasticity]], deregulates intercellular communication, and supports the development of [[grafo/termos/neuroinflammation|neuroinflammation]], thereby providing propagation of pathological events leading to the establishment of a PD-specific phenotype. Multidirectional and ambiguous effects of α-synuclein on adult neurogenesis suggest that impaired neurogenesis should be considered as a target for the prevention of cell loss and restoration of neurological functions. Thus, stimulation of endogenous neurogenesis or cell-replacement therapy with [[grafo/termos/stem cell-derived|stem cell-derived]] differentiated neurons raises new hopes for the development of effective and safe technologies for treating PD neurodegeneration. Given the rapid development of optogenetics, it is not surprising that this method has already been repeatedly tested in manipulating neurogenesis in vivo and in vitro via targeting stem or progenitor cells. However, niche [[grafo/termos/astrocytes|astrocytes]] could also serve as promising candidates for controlling neuronal differentiation and improving the [[grafo/termos/functional integration|functional integration]] of newly formed neurons within the [[grafo/termos/brain tissue|brain tissue]]. In this review, we mainly focus on current approaches to assess neurogenesis and prospects in the application of optogenetic protocols to restore the neurogenesis in Parkinson's disease.
