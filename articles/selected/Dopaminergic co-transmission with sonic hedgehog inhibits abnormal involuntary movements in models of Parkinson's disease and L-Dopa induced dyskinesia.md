[[grafo/termos/l-dopa-induced dyskinesia|L-Dopa induced dyskinesia]] ([[grafo/termos/levodopa-induced dyskinesia|LID]]) is a debilitating side effect of [[grafo/termos/dopamine replacement therapy|dopamine replacement therapy]] for Parkinson's Disease. The mechanistic underpinnings of LID remain obscure. Here we report that diminished sonic hedgehog (Shh) signaling in the [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia|basal ganglia]] caused by the degeneration of [[grafo/termos/midbrain dopamine neurons|midbrain dopamine neurons]] facilitates the formation and expression of LID. We find that the pharmacological activation of Smoothened, a downstream effector of Shh, attenuates LID in the neurotoxic 6-OHDA- and genetic aphakia mouse models of Parkinson's Disease. Employing conditional genetic loss-of-function approaches, we show that reducing Shh secretion from [[grafo/termos/dopamine neurons|dopamine neurons]] or Smoothened activity in [[grafo/termos/cholinergic interneurons|cholinergic interneurons]] promotes LID. Conversely, the selective expression of constitutively active Smoothened in cholinergic interneurons is sufficient to render the sensitized aphakia model of Parkinson's Disease resistant to LID. Furthermore, acute depletion of Shh from dopamine neurons through prolonged optogenetic stimulation in otherwise intact mice and in the absence of [[grafo/termos/l-dopa|L-Dopa]] produces LID-like [[grafo/termos/involuntary movements|involuntary movements]]. These findings indicate that augmenting Shh signaling in the L-Dopa treated brain may be a [[grafo/termos/promising therapeutic|promising therapeutic]] approach for mitigating the dyskinetic side effects of long-term treatment with L-Dopa.
