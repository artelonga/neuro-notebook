[[grafo/termos/l-dopa-induced dyskinesia|L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia]] ([[grafo/termos/levodopa-induced dyskinesia|LID]]) remains a major complication of Parkinson's disease management for which better therapies are necessary. The contribution of the striatonigral [[grafo/termos/direct pathway|direct pathway]] to LID is widely acknowledged but whether the striatopallidal pathway is involved remains debated. Selective optogenetic stimulation of striatonigral [[grafo/termos/axon terminals|axon terminals]] induces [[grafo/termos/dyskinesia|dyskinesia]] in mice rendered hemiparkinsonian with the toxin [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-hydroxydopamine]] (6-OHDA). Here we show that optogenetically-induced dyskinesia is increased by the D2-type [[grafo/termos/dopamine receptors|dopamine receptor]] agonist quinpirole. Although the quinpirole effect may be mediated by D2 receptor stimulation in [[grafo/termos/striatopallidal neurons|striatopallidal neurons]], alternative mechanisms may be responsible as well. To selectively modulate the striatopallidal pathway, we selectively expressed [[grafo/termos/channelrhodopsin-2|channelrhodopsin-2]] (ChR2) in D2 receptor expressing neurons by crossing D2-Cre and ChR2-flox mice. The animals were rendered hemiparkinsonian and implanted with an optic fiber at the ipsilateral [[grafo/termos/external globus pallidus|external globus pallidus]] (GPe). Stimulation of ChR2 at striatopallidal axon terminals reduced LID and also general motility during the off [[grafo/termos/l-dopa|L-DOPA]] state, without modifying the pro-motor effect of low doses of L-DOPA producing mild or no dyskinesia. Overall, the present study shows that D2-type dopamine receptors and the striatopallidal pathway modulate dyskinesia and suggest that targeting striatopallidal axon terminals at the GPe may have therapeutic potential in the management of LID.
