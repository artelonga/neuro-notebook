BACKGROUND: The pathophysiology of [[grafo/termos/levodopa-induced dyskinesia|levodopa-induced dyskinesia]] (LID) in Parkinson's disease (PD) is not well understood. Experimental data from numerous investigations support the idea that aberrant activity of D1 [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] receptor-positive [[grafo/termos/medium spiny neurons|medium spiny neurons]] in the striatal [[grafo/termos/direct pathway|direct pathway]] is associated with LID. However, a direct link between the real-time activity of these [[grafo/termos/striatal neurons|striatal neurons]] and dyskinetic symptoms remains to be established. METHODS: We examined the effect of acute [[grafo/termos/levodopa|levodopa]] treatment on striatal c-Fos expression in LID using D1-Cre PD rats with dyskinetic symptoms induced by chronic levodopa administration. We studied the real-time dynamics of striatal D1 + neurons during [[grafo/termos/dyskinetic behaviors|dyskinetic behavior]] using GCaMP6-based in vivo [[grafo/termos/fiber photometry|fiber photometry]]. We also examined the effects of striatal D1 + neuronal deactivation on [[grafo/termos/dyskinesia|dyskinesia]] in LID rats using optogenetics and [[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|chemogenetic]] methods. RESULTS: Striatal D1 + neurons in LID rats showed increased expression of c-Fos, a widely used marker for [[grafo/termos/neuronal activation|neuronal activation]], following levodopa injection. Fiber photometry revealed synchronized overactivity of striatal D1 + neurons during dyskinetic behavior in LID rats following levodopa administration. Consistent with these observations, optogenetic deactivation of striatal D1 + neurons was sufficient to inhibit most of the dyskinetic behaviors of LID animals. Moreover, [[grafo/termos/chemogenetic inhibition|chemogenetic inhibition]] of striatal D1 + neurons delayed the onset of dyskinetic behavior after levodopa administration. CONCLUSION: Our data demonstrated that aberrant activity of striatal D1 + [[grafo/termos/neuronal populations|neuronal population]] was causally linked with real-time dyskinetic symptoms in LID rats.
