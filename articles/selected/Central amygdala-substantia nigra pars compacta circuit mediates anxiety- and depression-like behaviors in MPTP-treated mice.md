Parkinson's disease (PD) is characterized by progressive degeneration of [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars compacta|substantia nigra pars compacta]] (SNc) [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] (DA) neurons, leading to [[grafo/termos/motor dysfunction|motor dysfunction]]. [[grafo/termos/non-motor symptoms|Non-motor symptoms]] of PD, such as [[grafo/termos/neuropsychiatric symptoms|neuropsychiatric symptoms]] ([[grafo/termos/anxiety|anxiety]] and [[grafo/termos/depression|depression]]), often precede [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptoms]] and seriously affect quality of life, but the mediating mechanism has not been well understood. The SNc receives input from somatostatin (SOM) neurons in the central amygdala (CeA), which convey [[grafo/termos/reward|reward]] related information. This study demonstrated that optogenetic activation of CeASOM-SNc circuit induced real-time place preference (RTPP) and alleviated anxiety-like behaviors caused by acute stress. SOM neurons in the CeA preferentially targeted [[grafo/termos/gabaergic neurons|GABAergic neurons]] in the SNc, optogenetic activation of CeASOM-SNc may activate TH neurons in the SNc through disinhibition. The response of CeA neurons to reward was blunted in MPTP-treated mice, and chronic [[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|chemogenetic]] silencing of CeA-SNc induced anxiety- and depression-like behaviors in mice. Furthermore, chronically [[grafo/termos/chemogenetic activation|chemogenetic activation]] of CeASOM-SNc or DA neurons in SNc alleviated anxiety- and depression-like behaviors in MPTP-treated mice. Reward-based chocolate intervention alleviated depression-like behaviors of MPTP-treated mice. In summary, our results indicate that the CeASOM-SNc circuit may mediate anxiety- and depression-like behaviors by regulating the activity of SNc DA neurons.
