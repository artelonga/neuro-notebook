Parkinson's disease (PD) is a [[grafo/termos/neurodegenerative disorders|neurodegenerative disorder]] characterized by a range of [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptoms]]. Treatments are focused on [[grafo/termos/dopamine replacement therapy|dopamine replacement therapy]] or [[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|deep brain stimulation]] (DBS). The [[grafo/termos/subthalamic nucleus|subthalamic nucleus]] (STN) is a common target for DBS treatment of PD. However, the function of the STN in normal conditions and pathology is [[grafo/termos/poorly understood|poorly understood]]. Here, we show in rats that optogenetic modulation of STN [[grafo/termos/neuronal activity|neuronal activity]] exerts bidirectional control of [[grafo/termos/motor function|motor function]], where inhibition of the STN increases movement and STN activation decreases movement. We also examined the effect of bidirectional optogenetic manipulation STN neuronal activity under [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] depleted condition using the bilateral rodent [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-hydroxydopamine]] (6-OHDA) model of Parkinson's disease. Optogenetic inhibition of the STN in the absence of dopamine had no impact on [[grafo/termos/motor control|motor control]] yet STN excitation led to pronounced abnormal [[grafo/termos/involuntary movements|involuntary movement]]. Administration of [[grafo/termos/levodopa|levodopa]] rescued the abnormal involuntary movements induced by STN excitation. Although dopamine and STN dysfunction are well established in PD pathology, here we demonstrate simultaneous STN over activity and loss of dopamine lead to [[grafo/termos/motor deficits|motor deficits]]. Moreover, we show the dysfunction of the STN is dependent on dopamine. This study provides evidence that the loss of dopamine and the over activity of the STN are key features of PD motor deficits. These results provide insight into the STN pathology in PD and [[grafo/termos/therapeutic mechanisms|therapeutic mechanism]] of targeting the STN for the treatment for PD.
