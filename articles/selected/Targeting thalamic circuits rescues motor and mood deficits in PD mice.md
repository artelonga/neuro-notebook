Although [[grafo/termos/bradykinesia|bradykinesia]], [[grafo/termos/tremor|tremor]] and rigidity are the hallmark motor defects in patients with Parkinson's disease (PD), patients also experience [[grafo/termos/motor learning|motor learning]] impairments and [[grafo/termos/non-motor symptoms|non-motor symptoms]] such as depression1. The [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuit]] basis for these different symptoms of PD are not well understood. Although current treatments are effective for [[grafo/termos/locomotion|locomotion]] deficits in PD2,3, [[grafo/termos/therapeutic strategies|therapeutic strategies]] targeting motor learning deficits and non-motor symptoms are lacking4-6. Here we found that distinct parafascicular (PF) thalamic subpopulations project to caudate putamen (CPu), [[grafo/termos/subthalamic nucleus|subthalamic nucleus]] (STN) and [[grafo/termos/nucleus accumbens|nucleus accumbens]] (NAc). Whereas PF→CPu and PF→STN circuits are critical for locomotion and motor learning, respectively, inhibition of the PF→NAc circuit induced a depression-like state. Whereas chemogenetically manipulating CPu-projecting PF neurons led to a long-term restoration of locomotion, optogenetic [[grafo/termos/long-term potentiation|long-term potentiation]] (LTP) at PF→STN [[grafo/termos/synapse|synapses]] restored motor learning behaviour in an acute mouse model of PD. Furthermore, activation of NAc-projecting PF neurons rescued depression-like phenotypes. Further, we identified [[grafo/termos/nicotinic acetylcholine receptors|nicotinic acetylcholine receptors]] capable of modulating PF circuits to rescue different PD phenotypes. Thus, targeting PF thalamic circuits may be an effective strategy for treating motor and non-motor deficits in PD.
