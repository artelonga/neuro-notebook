The changes of [[grafo/termos/local field potentials|local field potentials]] (LFP, mainly gamma rhythm and [[grafo/termos/theta rhythm|theta rhythm]]) in the brain are closely related to learning and [[grafo/termos/memory formation|memory formation]]. Reduced gamma rhythm (20-50 Hz) and theta rhythm (4-10 Hz) has been observed in the progression of Alzheimer's disease (AD), but it is not clear whether it is related to [[grafo/termos/cognition|cognition]] in AD. Here, we investigated behaviorally driven gamma rhythm and theta rhythm in APP/PS1 mice, and optogenetically stimulated [[grafo/termos/gabaergic neurons|GABAergic neurons]] in the brain to [[grafo/termos/better understand|better understand]] the relationship between the changes of LFP, cognition, and cellular pathologies. Optogenetically driving GABAergic neurons rescued memory formation in a [[grafo/termos/water maze|water maze]] task and normalized theta and gamma rhythm in the [[grafo/termos/eeg|EEG]]. Furthermore, the optogenetic stimulation alleviated [[grafo/termos/neuroinflammation|neuroinflammation]] and levels of [[grafo/termos/amyloid-β|amyloid-β]] (Aβ)1-42 fragments, and induced [[grafo/termos/autophagy|autophagy]]. [[grafo/termos/gaba|GABA]] blockers also reversed the normalization of theta and gamma rhythms in the brain by optogenetic stimulation. The results demonstrate that stimulation of [[grafo/termos/gabaergic interneurons|GABAergic interneurons]] not only rescues LFP rhythms and memory formation, but furthermore activates autophagy and reduces neuroinflammation, which have beneficial additional effects such as clearing amyloid. This is a proof of concept for a novel therapeutic approach to AD treatment.
