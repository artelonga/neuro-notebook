Pramipexole, a D2/D3 [[grafo/termos/dopamine receptors|dopamine receptor]] agonist, is used to treat the [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptoms]] of Parkinson's disease, caused by degeneration of the dopaminergic [[grafo/termos/nigrostriatal pathway|nigrostriatal pathway]]. There are three paradoxes associated with its mode of action. Firstly, stimulation of D2/D3 receptors leads to neuronal inhibition, although pramipexole does not inhibit but promotes some dopamine-modulated functions, such as [[grafo/termos/locomotion|locomotion]] and reinforcement. Secondly, another dopamine-modulated function, [[grafo/termos/arousal|arousal]], is not promoted but inhibited by pramipexole, leading to sedation. Thirdly, pramipexole-evoked sedation is associated with an increase in pupil diameter, although sedation is expected to cause pupil constriction. To resolve these paradoxes, the path from stimulation of D2/D3 receptors to the modification of dopamine-modulated functions has been tracked. The functions considered are modulated by midbrain dopaminergic nuclei: locomotion - [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars compacta|substantia nigra pars compacta]] (SNc), reinforcement/[[grafo/termos/motivation|motivation]] - [[grafo/termos/ventral tegmental area|ventral tegmental area]] (VTA), sympathetic activity (as reflected in pupil function) - VTA; arousal - ventral periaqueductal grey (vPAG), with contributions from VTA and SNc. The application of genetics-based molecular techniques (optogenetics and [[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|chemogenetics]]) has enabled tracing the chains of neurones from the dopaminergic nuclei to their final targets executing the functions. The functional [[grafo/termos/neuronal circuits|neuronal circuits]] linked to the D2/D3 receptors in the dorsal and ventral striata, stimulated by inputs from SNc and VTA, respectively, may explain how neuronal inhibition induced by pramipexole is translated into the promotion of locomotion, reinforcement/motivation and sympathetic activity. As the vPAG may increase arousal mainly by stimulating cortical D1 dopamine receptors, pramipexole would stimulate only presynaptic D2/D3 receptors on vPAG neurones, curtailing their activity and leading to sedation.
