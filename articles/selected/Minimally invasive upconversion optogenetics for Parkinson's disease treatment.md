The treatment of Parkinson's disease (PD) is greatly limited by the side effects of [[grafo/termos/levodopa|levodopa]], the current gold standard medication, which can cause severe [[grafo/termos/motor complications|motor complications]]. Optogenetic stimulation holds promise for PD therapy, but it has been plagued by invasive fiber insertion. Herein, we propose the minimally invasive treatment of PD using upconversion optogenetics for the first time. [[grafo/termos/channelrhodopsin-2|Channelrhodopsin-2]] (ChR2) is expressed in the [[grafo/termos/globus pallidus externus|globus pallidus externus]] ([[grafo/termos/external globus pallidus|GPe]]) [[grafo/termos/brain regions|brain region]] of PD mice, followed by in situ injection of upconversion nanoparticles (UCNPs). When near-infrared (NIR) light is delivered through a fiber fixed on the skull, UCNPs can convert NIR light into [[grafo/termos/blue light|blue light]], thereby effectively activating the GPe. Open field and rotarod tests demonstrate significant improvement in motor abilities and coordination in PD mice. Compared to conventional optogenetics, upconversion optogenetics not only achieves equivalent therapeutic effects but also avoids damage and inflammation caused by fiber insertion. Furthermore, this method can be extended to modulate [[grafo/termos/neural activity|neural activity]] in deep brain regions, including [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars compacta|substantia nigra pars compacta]] and paraventricular nucleus of the [[grafo/termos/thalamus|thalamus]], with neural activation effects lasting for up to one week. Our study presents a minimally invasive optogenetic strategy for PD treatment, with potential [[grafo/termos/therapeutic applications|therapeutic applications]] in various [[grafo/termos/neurological disorders|neurological disorders]].
