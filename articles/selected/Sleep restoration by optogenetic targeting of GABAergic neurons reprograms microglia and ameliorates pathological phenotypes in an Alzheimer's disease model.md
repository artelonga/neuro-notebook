BACKGROUND: Alzheimer's disease (AD) patients exhibit [[grafo/termos/memory|memory]] disruptions and profound [[grafo/termos/sleep disturbances|sleep disturbances]], including disruption of deep [[grafo/termos/non-rapid eye movement|non-rapid eye movement]] (NREM) [[grafo/termos/sleep|sleep]]. [[grafo/termos/slow-wave activity|Slow-wave activity]] (SWA) is a major restorative feature of [[grafo/termos/nrem sleep|NREM sleep]] and is important for [[grafo/termos/memory consolidation|memory consolidation]]. METHODS: We generated a mouse model where [[grafo/termos/gabaergic interneurons|GABAergic interneurons]] could be targeted in the presence of APPswe/PS1dE9 (APP) amyloidosis, APP-GAD-Cre mice. An electroencephalography ([[grafo/termos/eeg|EEG]]) / electromyography (EMG) telemetry system was used to monitor sleep disruptions in these animals. Optogenetic stimulation of GABAergic interneurons in the anterior cortex targeted with [[grafo/termos/channelrhodopsin-2|channelrhodopsin-2]] (ChR2) allowed us to examine the role GABAergic interneurons play in sleep deficits. We also examined the effect of optogenetic stimulation on [[grafo/termos/amyloid- plaques|amyloid plaques]], neuronal calcium as well as sleep-dependent memory consolidation. In addition, microglial morphological features and functions were assessed using [[grafo/termos/confocal microscopy|confocal microscopy]] and flow cytometry. Finally, we performed sleep deprivation during optogenetic stimulation to investigate whether sleep restoration was necessary to slow AD progression. RESULTS: APP-GAD-Cre mice exhibited impairments in sleep architecture including decreased time spent in NREM sleep, decreased delta power, and increased sleep fragmentation compared to nontransgenic (NTG) NTG-GAD-Cre mice. Optogenetic stimulation of cortical GABAergic interneurons increased SWA and rescued sleep impairments in APP-GAD-Cre animals. Furthermore, it slowed AD progression by reducing amyloid deposition, normalizing neuronal calcium homeostasis, and improving memory function. These changes were accompanied by increased numbers and a morphological transformation of [[grafo/termos/microglia|microglia]], elevated phagocytic marker expression, and enhanced [[grafo/termos/amyloid-β|amyloid β]] (Aβ) phagocytic activity of microglia. Sleep was necessary for amelioration of pathophysiological phenotypes in APP-GAD-Cre mice. CONCLUSIONS: In summary, our study shows that optogenetic targeting of GABAergic interneurons rescues sleep, which then ameliorates neuropathological as well as [[grafo/termos/behavioral deficits|behavioral deficits]] by increasing clearance of Aβ by microglia in an AD mouse model.
