Parkinson's disease is a complex neurodegenerative disease characterized by progressive movement impairments. Predominant symptoms encompass resting [[grafo/termos/tremor|tremor]], [[grafo/termos/bradykinesia|bradykinesia]], limb rigidity, and postural instability. In addition, it also includes a series of [[grafo/termos/non-motor symptoms|non-motor symptoms]] such as [[grafo/termos/sleep disorders|sleep disorders]], hyposmia, gastrointestinal dysfunction, autonomic dysfunction and [[grafo/termos/cognitive impairment|cognitive impairment]]. Pathologically, the disease manifests through dopaminergic [[grafo/termos/neuronal loss|neuronal loss]] and the presence of [[grafo/termos/lewy bodies|Lewy bodies]]. At present, no significant breakthrough has been achieved in clinical Parkinson's disease treatment. Exploring treatment modalities necessitate the establishment of scientifically sound animal models. In recent years, researchers have focused on replicating the symptoms of human Parkinson's disease, resulting in the establishment of various experimental animal models primarily through drugs and transgenic methods to mimic relevant pathologies and identify more [[grafo/termos/effective treatments|effective treatments]]. This review examines traditional neurotoxin and transgenic animal models as well as [[grafo/termos/α-synuclein|α-synuclein]] pre-formed fibrils models, [[grafo/termos/nonhuman primates|non-human primate]] models and non-mammalian specie models. Additionally, it introduces emerging models, including models based on optogenetics, [[grafo/termos/induced pluripotent stem cells|induced pluripotent stem cells]], and [[grafo/termos/gene editing|gene editing]], aiming to provide a reference for the utilization of experimental animal models and clinical research for researchers in this field.
