Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a severe and [[grafo/termos/progressive neurodegenerative|progressive neurodegenerative]] condition that exerts detrimental effects on [[grafo/termos/brain function|brain function]]. As of now, there is no [[grafo/termos/effective treatments|effective treatment]] for AD patients. This review explores two distinct avenues of research. The first revolves around the use of animal studies and preclinical models to gain insights into AD's [[grafo/termos/underlying mechanisms|underlying mechanisms]] and potential treatment strategies. Specifically, it delves into the effectiveness of interventions such as Optogenetics and [[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|Chemogenetics]], shedding light on their implications for understanding [[grafo/termos/pathophysiological mechanisms|pathophysiological mechanisms]] and potential [[grafo/termos/therapeutic applications|therapeutic applications]]. The second avenue focuses on non-invasive [[grafo/termos/brain stimulation|brain stimulation]] (NiBS) techniques in the context of AD. Evidence suggests that NiBS can successfully modulate [[grafo/termos/cognitive function|cognitive functions]] associated with various neurological and [[grafo/termos/neuropsychiatric disorders|neuropsychiatric disorders]], including AD, as demonstrated by promising findings. Here, we critically assessed recent findings in AD research belonging to these lines of research and discuss their potential impact on the clinical horizon of AD treatment. These multifaceted approaches offer hope for advancing our comprehension of AD pathology and developing novel [[grafo/termos/therapeutic interventions|therapeutic interventions]].
