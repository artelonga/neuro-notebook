[[grafo/termos/parvalbumin|Parvalbumin]] expressing (PV+) [[grafo/termos/gabaergic interneurons|GABAergic interneurons]] are fast spiking neurons that provide powerful but relatively short-lived inhibition to principal excitatory cells in the brain. They play a vital role in feedforward and feedback synaptic inhibition, preventing run away excitation in [[grafo/termos/neural network|neural networks]]. Hence, their dysfunction can lead to hyperexcitability and increased susceptibility to seizures. PV+ interneurons are also key players in generating [[grafo/termos/gamma oscillations|gamma oscillations]], which are synchronized [[grafo/termos/neural oscillations|neural oscillations]] associated with various [[grafo/termos/cognitive function|cognitive functions]]. PV+ interneuron are particularly vulnerable to aging and their degeneration has been associated with [[grafo/termos/cognitive decline|cognitive decline]] and [[grafo/termos/memory impairment|memory impairment]] in dementia and Alzheimer's disease (AD). Overall, dysfunction of PV+ interneurons disrupts the normal excitatory/inhibitory balance within specific neurocircuits in the brain and thus has been linked to a [[grafo/termos/wide range|wide range]] of neurodevelopmental and [[grafo/termos/neuropsychiatric disorders|neuropsychiatric disorders]]. This review focuses on the role of dysfunctional PV+ [[grafo/termos/inhibitory interneurons|inhibitory interneurons]] in the generation of epileptic seizures and [[grafo/termos/cognitive impairment|cognitive impairment]] and their potential as targets in the design of [[grafo/termos/future therapeutic|future therapeutic]] strategies to treat these disorders. Recent research using cutting-edge optogenetic and [[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|chemogenetic]] technologies has demonstrated that they can be selectively manipulated to control seizures and restore the balance of [[grafo/termos/neural activity|neural activity]] in the brains of animal models. This suggests that PV+ interneurons could be important targets in developing future treatments for patients with [[grafo/termos/epilepsy|epilepsy]] and comorbid disorders, such as AD, where seizures and cognitive decline are directly linked to specific PV+ interneuron deficits.
