Presynaptic accumulation of misfolded [[grafo/termos/α-synuclein|α-synuclein]] (α-syn) and altered [[grafo/termos/synaptic transmission|synaptic transmission]] are considered early events in the pathogenesis of Parkinson's disease (PD), suggesting a potential causal link between these two events. However, the mechanisms by which α-syn aggregation induces [[grafo/termos/synaptic dysfunction|synaptic dysfunction]] and the subsequent progressive [[grafo/termos/neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]] remain elusive. In the present study we leveraged the high [[grafo/termos/temporal resolution|temporal resolution]] of the [[grafo/termos/light-inducible protein aggregation|Light-Inducible Protein Aggregation]] (LIPA) system in vivo and in human [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]] to explore the early sequence of α-syn-induced pathological events leading to synaptopathy. We observed that nigrostriatal axonal transport and presynaptic accumulation of α-syn aggregates altered the activity of different [[grafo/termos/neuronal populations|neuronal populations]] in the mouse [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]]. The results of histological and metabolite analyses show that presynaptic accumulation of α-syn induced a shift in the activation pattern of D1- and D2-expressing [[grafo/termos/striatal medium spiny neurons|striatal medium spiny neurons]], caused an increase in the size and density of dopaminergic [[grafo/termos/synapse|synapses]], and disrupted [[grafo/termos/striatal dopamine|striatal dopamine]] signaling. Altogether, our findings reveal that the accumulation of α-syn in dopaminergic terminals triggered early presynaptic impairments, which subsequently altered striatal [[grafo/termos/neuronal activity|neuronal activity]]. Our study provides new insights into the [[grafo/termos/molecular mechanisms|molecular mechanisms]] underlying early synaptopathy in PD.
