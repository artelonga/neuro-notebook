BACKGROUND: Medial temporal lobe hyperexcitation or seizures originating from the [[grafo/termos/hippocampus|hippocampus]] are frequently observed in Alzheimer's disease (AD) patients, contributing to accelerated [[grafo/termos/cognitive decline|cognitive decline]]. As the hippocampus is an early vulnerable area of [[grafo/termos/tau pathology|tau pathology]], the mechanisms by which abnormal [[grafo/termos/tau aggregation|tau aggregation]] promotes [[grafo/termos/temporal lobe epilepsy|temporal lobe epilepsy]] (TLE) remain [[grafo/termos/poorly understood|poorly understood]]. METHODS: We investigated the role of AD-like hippocampal tau aggregation in neuronal hyperexcitation using transgenic (Tg) tau-driven mice. Effects of tau aggregation on intracellular calcium dynamics were assessed by [[grafo/termos/calcium imaging|calcium imaging]]. Neuronal/network hyperexcitability and seizure susceptibility were evaluated through patch-clamp [[grafo/termos/electrophysiology|electrophysiology]], 18F-FDG PET/CT, and optogenetic induction. A tetracycline-controlled (Tet-on) system in Tg hTau368 mice enabled spatiotemporal induction of tau pathology to investigate its interactions with calbindin-D28k (CB) and [[grafo/termos/synaptic proteins|synaptic proteins]]. [[grafo/termos/adeno-associated virus|Adeno-associated virus]] (AAV)-mediated CB supplementation in [[grafo/termos/hippocampal ca1|hippocampal CA1]] and [[grafo/termos/dentate gyrus|dentate gyrus]] (DG) [[grafo/termos/excitatory neurons|excitatory neurons]] was performed to correct hyperexcitability and [[grafo/termos/cognitive deficits|cognitive deficits]]. Finally, the relationship between CB and disease progress was analyzed using an AD public database. RESULTS: [[grafo/termos/tau accumulation|Tau accumulation]] in the hippocampal CA1/DG CaMKII-positive excitatory neurons reduced CB expression with disruption of calcium homeostasis. This dysregulation increased [[grafo/termos/neuronal excitability|neuronal excitability]], diminished synaptic protein levels, and increased seizure susceptibility and [[grafo/termos/cognitive impairment|cognitive impairment]]. AAV-driven CB restoration in CA1/DG neurons attenuated both hyperexcitability and cognitive deficits. In the brains of AD patients, reduced CB expression was associated with cognitive deterioration and advanced disease stages. CONCLUSIONS: Tau aggregation drives calcium dysregulation and hippocampal neuronal hyperexcitation through reducing CB expression. These results establish a potential mechanistic link between [[grafo/termos/tauopathy|tauopathy]] and TLE pathogenesis in AD, providing evidence for CB as a [[grafo/termos/promising therapeutic|promising therapeutic]] target for mitigating seizure risk and related cognitive decline in AD.
