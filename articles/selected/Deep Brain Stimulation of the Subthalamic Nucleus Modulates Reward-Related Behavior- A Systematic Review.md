[[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|Deep brain stimulation]] of the [[grafo/termos/subthalamic nucleus|subthalamic nucleus]] (STN-DBS) is an [[grafo/termos/effective treatments|effective treatment]] for the [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptoms]] of [[grafo/termos/movement disorders|movement disorders]] including Parkinson's Disease (PD). Despite its [[grafo/termos/therapeutic benefits|therapeutic benefits]], STN-DBS has been associated with adverse effects on mood and [[grafo/termos/cognition|cognition]]. Specifically, apathy, which is defined as a loss of [[grafo/termos/motivation|motivation]], has been reported to emerge or to worsen following STN-DBS. However, it is often challenging to disentangle the effects of STN-DBS per se from concurrent reduction of [[grafo/termos/dopamine replacement therapy|dopamine replacement therapy]], from underlying PD pathology or from disease progression. To this end, pre-clinical models allow for the dissociation of each of these factors, and to establish [[grafo/termos/neural substrates|neural substrates]] underlying the emergence of motivational symptoms following STN-DBS. Here, we performed a systematic analysis of rodent studies assessing the effects of STN-DBS on [[grafo/termos/reward seeking|reward seeking]], [[grafo/termos/reward|reward]] motivation and reward consumption across a variety of behavioral paradigms. We find that STN-DBS decreases reward seeking in the majority of experiments, and we outline how design of the behavioral task and DBS parameters can influence experimental outcomes. While an early hypothesis posited that DBS acts as a "functional lesion," an analysis of lesions and inhibition of the STN revealed no consistent pattern on reward-related behavior. Thus, we discuss alternative mechanisms that could contribute to the amotivational effects of STN-DBS. We also argue that optogenetic-assisted circuit dissection could yield important insight into the effects of the STN on motivated behavior in health and disease. Understanding the [[grafo/termos/mechanisms underlying|mechanisms underlying]] the effects of STN-DBS on motivated behavior-will be critical for optimizing the [[grafo/termos/clinical applications|clinical application]] of STN-DBS.
