[[grafo/termos/lewy body|Lewy body]] diseases, including Parkinson's disease (PD), are characterized by the spread of [[grafo/termos/alpha-synuclein|alpha-synuclein]] (αSyn) between neurons across [[grafo/termos/synapse|synapses]], a process crucial for understanding their pathophysiology and developing [[grafo/termos/effective treatments|effective treatments]]. In this study, we aimed to investigate the role of [[grafo/termos/neuronal activity|neuronal activity]] in releasing αSyn from human induced pluripotent [[grafo/termos/stem cell-derived|stem cell-derived]] [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]]. We examined human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived dopaminergic neurons, both healthy and those with the αSyn gene mutation associated with PD. We employed pharmacological agents and optogenetic techniques and demonstrated that increased neuronal activity, induced by bicuculline or optogenetic stimulation, significantly enhances αSyn release. However, suppression of neuronal activity with cyanquixaline reduces αSyn secretion. These findings underscore the pivotal role of neuronal activity in αSyn transmission between neurons, showing its potential impact on the spread of Lewy pathology in patients with neurodegenerative diseases like PD. Therefore, this study advances our understanding of PD and opens new avenues for [[grafo/termos/therapeutic strategies|therapeutic strategies]] to mitigate Lewy body disease progression.
