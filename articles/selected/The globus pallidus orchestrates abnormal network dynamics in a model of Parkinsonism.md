The dynamical properties of cortico-basal ganglia (CBG) circuits are dramatically altered following the loss of [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] in Parkinson's disease (PD). The [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuit]] dysfunctions associated with PD include spike-rate alteration concomitant with excessive oscillatory spike-synchronization in the [[grafo/termos/beta frequency|beta frequency]] range (12-30 Hz). Which [[grafo/termos/neuronal circuits|neuronal circuits]] orchestrate and propagate these abnormal neural dynamics in CBG remains unknown. In this work, we combine in [[grafo/termos/vivo electrophysiological|vivo electrophysiological]] recordings with advanced optogenetic manipulations in normal and [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-OHDA]] rats to [[grafo/termos/shed light|shed light]] on the mechanistic principle underlying circuit dysfunction in PD. Our results show that abnormal neural dynamics present in a [[grafo/termos/rat model|rat model]] of PD do not rely on cortical or [[grafo/termos/subthalamic nucleus|subthalamic nucleus]] activity but critically dependent on [[grafo/termos/globus pallidus|globus pallidus]] (GP) integrity. Our findings highlight the pivotal role played by the GP which operates as a hub nucleus capable of orchestrating [[grafo/termos/firing rate|firing rate]] and synchronization changes across CBG circuits both in normal and pathological conditions.
