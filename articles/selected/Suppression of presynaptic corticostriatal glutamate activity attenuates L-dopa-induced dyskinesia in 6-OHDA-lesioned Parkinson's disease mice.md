A common adverse effect of Parkinson's disease (PD) treatment is [[grafo/termos/l-dopa-induced dyskinesia|L-dopa-induced dyskinesia]] ([[grafo/termos/levodopa-induced dyskinesia|LID]]). This condition results from both [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] (DA)-dependent and DA-independent mechanisms, as [[grafo/termos/glutamate|glutamate]] inputs from corticostriatal [[grafo/termos/projection neurons|projection neurons]] impact DA-responsive [[grafo/termos/medium spiny neurons|medium spiny neurons]] in the [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]] to cause the [[grafo/termos/dyskinetic behaviors|dyskinetic behaviors]]. In this study, we explored whether suppression of presynaptic corticostriatal glutamate inputs might affect the behavioral and biochemical outcomes associated with LID. We first established an animal model in which [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-hydroxydopamine]] (6-OHDA)-lesioned mice were treated daily with [[grafo/termos/l-dopa|L-dopa]] (10 mg/kg, i.p.) for 2 weeks; these mice developed stereotypical abnormal [[grafo/termos/involuntary movements|involuntary movements]] (AIMs). When the mice were pretreated with the NMDA antagonist, amantadine, we observed suppression of AIMs and reductions of phosphorylated ERK1/2 and NR2B in the striatum. We then took an optogenetic approach to manipulate glutamatergic activity. Slc17a6 ([[grafo/termos/vglut2|vGluT2]])-Cre mice were injected with pAAV5-Ef1a-DIO-eNpHR3.0-mCherry and received optic fiber implants in either the M1 [[grafo/termos/motor cortex|motor cortex]] or [[grafo/termos/dorsolateral striatum|dorsolateral striatum]]. Optogenetic inactivation at either optic fiber implant location could successfully reduce the intensity of AIMs after 6-OHDA lesioning and [[grafo/termos/l-dopa treatment|L-dopa treatment]]. Both optical manipulation strategies also suppressed phospho-ERK1/2 and phospho-NR2B signals in the striatum. Finally, we performed intrastriatal injections of LDN 212320 in the dyskenesic mice to enhance expression of [[grafo/termos/glutamate uptake|glutamate uptake]] transporter GLT-1. Sixteen hours after the LDN 212320 treatment, L-dopa-induced AIMs were reduced along with the levels of striatal phospho-ERK1/2 and phospho-NR2B. Together, our results affirm a critical role of corticostriatal glutamate neurons in LID and strongly suggest that diminishing synaptic glutamate, either by suppression of [[grafo/termos/neuronal activity|neuronal activity]] or by upregulation of GLT-1, could be an effective approach for managing LID.
