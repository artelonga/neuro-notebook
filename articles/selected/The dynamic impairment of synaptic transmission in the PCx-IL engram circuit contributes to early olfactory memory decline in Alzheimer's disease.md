Olfactory dysfunction has emerged as a promising target for the early diagnosis and treatment of Alzheimer's disease (AD). However, the [[grafo/termos/mechanisms underlying|mechanisms underlying]] [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuit]] disruption associated with olfactory dysfunction in AD remain [[grafo/termos/poorly understood|poorly understood]]. We conducted single-cell RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and ex [[grafo/termos/vivo electrophysiological|vivo electrophysiological]] studies to determine the link between olfactory [[grafo/termos/memory|memory]] in AD and dynamic [[grafo/termos/synaptic transmission|synaptic transmission]] disorders in PCx-IL [[grafo/termos/engram cells|engram cell]] circuits. Clinical [[grafo/termos/functional magnetic resonance|functional magnetic resonance]] imaging (fMRI) data revealed that connectivity between the piriform cortex (PCx) and the infralimbic cortex (IL) was impaired during the early [[grafo/termos/mild cognitive impairment|mild cognitive impairment]] (MCI) stage of AD. Optogenetic stimulation of IL-projecting PCx engram neurons successfully improved olfactory [[grafo/termos/memory retrieval|memory retrieval]] in 5xFAD mice. In addition, single-cell RNA sequencing was employed to investigate the mechanisms of damage in IL engram cells, which revealed increased [[grafo/termos/glutamate|glutamate]] expression and impaired synaptic function as key alterations. Guided by single-cell sequencing data, we analyzed glutamatergic synaptic transmission in the PCx-IL engram cell circuit in 5xFAD mice. These results indicated dynamic impairments in AMPA receptor-associated synaptic transmission within this circuit. Optical [[grafo/termos/long-term potentiation|long-term potentiation]] (LTP) of synaptic transmission restored directional engram synaptic transmission and prevented olfactory memory decline. Therefore, dynamic impairment of synaptic transmission in the PCx-IL engram cell circuit underlies the early decline in olfactory memory in AD. Impairment of PCx-IL [[grafo/termos/functional connectivity|functional connectivity]] may represent a new target for the diagnosis and treatment of [[grafo/termos/early stages|early-stage]] AD.
