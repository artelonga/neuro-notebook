Alzheimer's disease (AD) is the major cause of dementia, characterized by the presence of [[grafo/termos/amyloid beta|amyloid-beta]] plaques and neurofibrillary [[grafo/termos/tau tangles|tau tangles]]. Plaques and tangles are associated with [[grafo/termos/sleep-wake cycles|sleep-wake cycle]] disruptions, including the disruptions in [[grafo/termos/non-rapid eye movement|non-rapid eye movement]] (NREM) [[grafo/termos/slow waves|slow wave]] [[grafo/termos/sleep|sleep]] (SWS). Alzheimer's patients spend less time in [[grafo/termos/nrem sleep|NREM sleep]] and exhibit decreased [[grafo/termos/slow-wave activity|slow wave activity]] (SWA). Consistent with the critical role of SWS in [[grafo/termos/memory consolidation|memory consolidation]], reduced SWA is associated with impaired memory consolidation in AD patients. The aberrant SWA can be modeled in transgenic mouse models of amyloidosis and [[grafo/termos/tauopathy|tauopathy]]. Animal models exhibited slow wave impairments early in the disease progression, prior to the deposition of amyloid-beta plaques, however, in the presence of abundant oligomeric amyloid-beta. Optogenetic rescue of SWA successfully halted the amyloid accumulation and restored intraneuronal calcium levels in mice. On the other hand, optogenetic acceleration of slow wave frequency exacerbated amyloid deposition and disrupted neuronal calcium homeostasis. In this review, we summarize the evidence and the [[grafo/termos/mechanisms underlying|mechanisms underlying]] the existence of a positive feedback loop between amyloid/[[grafo/termos/tau pathology|tau pathology]] and SWA disruptions that lead to further accumulations of amyloid and [[grafo/termos/tau|tau]] in AD. Moreover, since SWA disruptions occur prior to the plaque deposition, SWA disruptions may provide an early biomarker for AD. Finally, we propose that therapeutic targeting of SWA in AD might lead to an [[grafo/termos/effective treatments|effective treatment]] for Alzheimer's patients.
