Increasing prevalence in the near future of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) has been statistically predicted. As current [[grafo/termos/pharmacological treatments|pharmacological treatments]] turn out to be limited for the long-term clinical effects accompanying various side effects and complications, new schemes are in urgent need of a relatively safer and more [[grafo/termos/effective treatments|effective treatment]]. In recent years, [[grafo/termos/neuromodulation|neuromodulation]] technologies such as optogenetics, [[grafo/termos/transcranial magnetic stimulation|transcranial magnetic stimulation]] (TMS), [[grafo/termos/vagus nerve|vagus nerve]] stimulation (VNS), transcranial [[grafo/termos/electrical stimulation|electrical stimulation]] (tES), [[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|deep brain stimulation]] (DBS), and [[grafo/termos/focused ultrasound|focused ultrasound]] (FUS), which are proven with potential remedial effectiveness in several mental diseases, emerge to be a prospective [[grafo/termos/therapeutic strategy|therapeutic strategy]] for AD. By applying optical, magnetic, as well as electronic technology, modulations on specific [[grafo/termos/brain regions|brain regions]] can be achieved. And by applying ultrasonic technology, the process of medical molecules passing through the brain-blood barrier (BBB) can be facilitated. This article summarizes the current knowledge of some main neuromodulation technologies and provides insight into their possibilities for [[grafo/termos/clinical applications|clinical application]]. By further illuminating the clinical effects, advantages, and limitations of these technologies, the article may help to guide clinical practice and enlighten new ideas for therapeutic strategy in AD.
