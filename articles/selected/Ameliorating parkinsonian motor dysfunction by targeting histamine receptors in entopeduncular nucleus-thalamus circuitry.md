In Parkinson's disease (PD), reduced [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] levels in the [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia|basal ganglia]] have been associated with altered [[grafo/termos/neuronal firing|neuronal firing]] and [[grafo/termos/motor dysfunction|motor dysfunction]]. It [[grafo/termos/remains unclear|remains unclear]] whether the altered [[grafo/termos/firing rate|firing rate]] or pattern of basal ganglia neurons leads to parkinsonism-associated motor dysfunction. In the present study, we show that increased histaminergic innervation of the [[grafo/termos/entopeduncular nucleus|entopeduncular nucleus]] (EPN) in the mouse model of PD leads to activation of EPN [[grafo/termos/parvalbumin|parvalbumin]] (PV) neurons projecting to the thalamic motor nucleus via hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated (HCN) channels coupled to postsynaptic H2R. Simultaneously, this effect is negatively regulated by presynaptic H3R activation in [[grafo/termos/subthalamic nucleus|subthalamic nucleus]] (STN) [[grafo/termos/glutamatergic neurons|glutamatergic neurons]] projecting to the EPN. Notably, the activation of both types of receptors ameliorates parkinsonism-associated motor dysfunction. Pharmacological activation of H2R or genetic upregulation of HCN2 in EPNPV neurons, which reduce neuronal burst firing, ameliorates parkinsonism-associated motor dysfunction independent of changes in the neuronal firing rate. In addition, optogenetic inhibition of EPNPV neurons and pharmacological activation or genetic upregulation of H3R in EPN-projecting STNGlu neurons ameliorate parkinsonism-associated motor dysfunction by reducing the firing rate rather than altering the [[grafo/termos/firing patterns|firing pattern]] of EPNPV neurons. Thus, although a reduced firing rate and more regular firing pattern of EPNPV neurons correlate with amelioration in parkinsonism-associated motor dysfunction, the firing pattern appears to be more critical in this context. These results also confirm that targeting H2R and its downstream HCN2 channel in EPNPV neurons and H3R in EPN-projecting STNGlu neurons may represent potential [[grafo/termos/therapeutic strategies|therapeutic strategies]] for the clinical treatment of parkinsonism-associated motor dysfunction.
