Plethora of research has [[grafo/termos/shed light|shed light]] on the critical role of [[grafo/termos/synaptic dysfunction|synaptic dysfunction]] in various [[grafo/termos/neurodegenerative disorders|neurodegenerative disorders]] (NDDs), including Alzheimer's disease (AD), Parkinson's disease (PD), [[grafo/termos/amyotrophic lateral sclerosis|Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis]] (ALS), and Huntington's disease (HD). [[grafo/termos/synapse|Synapses]], the fundamental units for neural communication in the brain, are highly vulnerable to pathological conditions and are central to the progression of neurological diseases. The presynaptic terminal, a key component of synapses responsible for neurotransmitter release and synaptic communication, undergoes structural and [[grafo/termos/functional alterations|functional alterations]] in these disorders. Understanding [[grafo/termos/synaptic transmission|synaptic transmission]] abnormalities is crucial for unravelling the [[grafo/termos/pathophysiological mechanisms|pathophysiological mechanisms]] underlying [[grafo/termos/neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]]. In the quest to probe synaptic transmission in NDDs, emerging biophysical techniques play a pivotal role. These advanced methods offer insights into the structural and functional changes occurring at nerve terminals in conditions like AD, PD, HD & ALS. By investigating [[grafo/termos/synaptic plasticity|synaptic plasticity]] and alterations in neurotransmitter release dynamics, researchers can uncover valuable information about disease progression and potential [[grafo/termos/therapeutic target|therapeutic targets]]. The review articles highlighted provide a comprehensive overview of how synaptic vulnerability and pathology are shared mechanisms across a spectrum of [[grafo/termos/neurological disorders|neurological disorders]]. In major neurodegenerative diseases, synaptic dysfunction is a common thread linking these conditions. The intricate molecular machinery involved in neurotransmitter release, synaptic vesicle dynamics, and presynaptic protein regulation are key areas of focus for understanding synaptic alterations in neurodegenerative diseases.
