Inter-brain region activity coupling is essential for enabling coordinated neural communication, facilitating complex brain processes including [[grafo/termos/motor behaviors|motor behaviors]]. In Parkinson's Disease (PD) patients, cortico-striatal decoupling is widely reported while its onset and [[grafo/termos/cellular mechanisms|cellular mechanism]] remain largely unclear. Using dual-site [[grafo/termos/fiber photometry|fiber photometry]] and Cre transgenic mouse lines, we examined activity coordination between the primary motor (M1) cortex and [[grafo/termos/dorsal striatum|dorsal striatum]] (cortico-striatal coupling) with [[grafo/termos/cell types|cell-type]] resolution. This method identifies motor behavior-specific coupling patterns with different contributions from striatal D1R- and D2R-expressing [[grafo/termos/medium spiny neurons|medium spiny neurons]] (MSNs). In an [[grafo/termos/α-synuclein|α-synuclein]] preformed fibrils (PFF) induced PD-like mouse model, cortico-striatal coupling associated with digging behavior is selectively disrupted as early as two months post-induction, whose progressive deterioration correlates with later-onset [[grafo/termos/behavioral deficits|behavioral deficits]]. Optogenetic disruption or enhancement of cortico-striatal coupling is sufficient to induce digging deficits in wild-type mice or rescue such deficits in PFF-injected mice, respectively. Furthermore, such decoupling is mainly mediated by impaired D1R MSNs, which can be rescued by D1 [[grafo/termos/receptor activation|receptor activation]] or [[grafo/termos/l-dopa|L-DOPA]]. These findings demonstrate that early-onset, behavior- and [[grafo/termos/cell-type specific|cell type-specific]] cortico-striatal decoupling emerges early during the development of PD-like symptoms.
