The [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]] integrates dopaminergic and glutamatergic inputs to select preferred versus alternative actions. However, the precise [[grafo/termos/mechanisms underlying|mechanisms underlying]] this process remain unclear. One way to study [[grafo/termos/action selection|action selection]] is to understand how it breaks down in pathological states. Here, we explored the cellular and synaptic mechanisms of [[grafo/termos/levodopa-induced dyskinesia|levodopa-induced dyskinesia]] (LID), a complication of Parkinson's disease therapy characterized by [[grafo/termos/involuntary movements|involuntary movements]]. We used an activity-dependent tool (FosTRAP) in conjunction with a mouse model of LID to investigate functionally distinct subsets of striatal [[grafo/termos/direct pathway|direct pathway]] [[grafo/termos/medium spiny neurons|medium spiny neurons]] (dMSNs). In vivo, [[grafo/termos/levodopa|levodopa]] differentially activates dyskinesia-associated (TRAPed) dMSNs compared to other dMSNs. We found this differential activation of TRAPed dMSNs is likely to be driven by higher [[grafo/termos/dopamine receptors|dopamine receptor]] expression, dopamine-dependent excitability, and excitatory input from the [[grafo/termos/motor cortex|motor cortex]] and [[grafo/termos/thalamus|thalamus]]. Together, these findings suggest how the intrinsic and [[grafo/termos/synaptic properties|synaptic properties]] of heterogeneous dMSN subpopulations integrate to support action selection.
