Transcranial [[grafo/termos/brain stimulation|brain stimulation]] is a promising technology for safe modulation of [[grafo/termos/brain function|brain function]] without invasive procedures. Recent advances in transcranial optogenetic techniques with external [[grafo/termos/light sources|light sources]], using upconversion particles and highly sensitive opsins, have shown promise for precise [[grafo/termos/neuromodulation|neuromodulation]] with improved [[grafo/termos/spatial resolution|spatial resolution]] in deeper [[grafo/termos/brain regions|brain regions]]. However, these methods have not yet been used to selectively excite or [[grafo/termos/inhibit specific|inhibit specific]] [[grafo/termos/neural population|neural populations]] in multiple brain regions. In this study, we created a wireless transcranial optogenetic brain modulator that combines highly sensitive opsins and upconversion particles and allows for precise bimodal neuromodulation of multiple brain regions without optical crosstalk. We demonstrate the feasibility of our approach in freely behaving mice. Furthermore, we demonstrate its usefulness in studies of complex behaviors and brain dysfunction by controlling extorting behavior in mice in food competition tests and alleviating the symptoms of Parkinson's disease. Our approach has potential applications in the study of [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuits]] and development of treatments for various [[grafo/termos/brain disorders|brain disorders]].
