Parkinson's disease (PD) is characterized by the degeneration of [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]] in the [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra|substantia nigra]] and leads to progressive [[grafo/termos/motor dysfunction|motor dysfunction]]. While studies have focused on the [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia|basal ganglia]] network, recent evidence suggests neuronal systems outside the basal ganglia are also related to PD pathogenesis. The [[grafo/termos/zona incerta|zona incerta]] (ZI) is a predominantly inhibitory subthalamic region for global behavioral modulation. This study investigates the role of [[grafo/termos/gabaergic neurons|GABAergic neurons]] in the ZI in a mouse model of [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-hydroxydopamine]] (6-OHDA)-induced PD. First, we found a decrease in GABA-positive neurons in the ZI, and then the mice used [[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|chemogenetic]]/optogenetic stimulation to activate or inhibit GABAergic neurons. The motor performance of PD mice was significantly improved by chemogenetic/optogenetic activation of GABAergic neurons, and repeated [[grafo/termos/chemogenetic activation|chemogenetic activation]] of ZI GABAergic neurons increased the [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] content in the [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]]. Our work identifies the role of ZI GABAergic neurons in regulating [[grafo/termos/motor behaviors|motor behaviors]] in 6-OHDA-lesioned PD model mice.
