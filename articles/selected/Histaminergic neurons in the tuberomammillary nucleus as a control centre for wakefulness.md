[[grafo/termos/histamine|Histamine]] plays pleiotropic roles as a neurotransmitter in the physiology of [[grafo/termos/brain function|brain function]], this includes the maintenance of wakefulness, appetite regulation and [[grafo/termos/memory retrieval|memory retrieval]]. Since numerous studies have revealed an association between histaminergic dysfunction and diverse [[grafo/termos/neuropsychiatric disorders|neuropsychiatric disorders]], such as Alzheimer's disease and schizophrenia, a large number of compounds acting on the brain histamine system have been developed to treat [[grafo/termos/neurological disorders|neurological disorders]]. In 2016, pitolisant, which was developed as a histamine H3 receptor inverse agonist by Schwartz and colleagues, was launched for the treatment of narcolepsy, emphasising the prominent role of brain histamine on wakefulness. Recent advances in neuroscientific techniques such as [[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|chemogenetic]] and optogenetic approaches have led to remarkable progress in the understanding of histaminergic [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuits]] essential for the control of wakefulness. In this review article, we summarise the basic knowledge about the histaminergic nervous system and the [[grafo/termos/mechanisms underlying|mechanisms underlying]] [[grafo/termos/sleep|sleep]]/wake regulation that are controlled by the brain histamine system.
