[[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurotransmission|Dopaminergic neurotransmission]] is involved in several important [[grafo/termos/brain function|brain functions]], such as [[grafo/termos/motor control|motor control]], learning, reward-motivated behavior, and emotions. Dysfunctions of dopaminergic system may lead to the development of various neurological and [[grafo/termos/psychiatric disorders|psychiatric disorders]], like Parkinson's disease, schizophrenia, [[grafo/termos/depression|depression]], and [[grafo/termos/addiction|addictions]]. Despite years of sustained research, it is not fully established how dopaminergic neurotransmission governs these important functions through a relatively small number of neurons that release [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]]. Light-driven neurotechnologies, based on the use of small light-regulated molecules or overexpression of light-regulated proteins in neurons, have greatly contributed to the advancement of our understanding of dopaminergic circuits and our ability to control them selectively. Here, we overview the current state-of-the-art of light-driven control of dopaminergic neurotransmission. While we provide a concise guideline for the readers interested in pharmacological, pharmacogenetic, and optogenetic approaches to modulate dopaminergic neurotransmission, our primary focus is on the usage of photocaged and photo-switchable small dopaminergic molecules. We argue that photopharmacology, photoswitchable molecules of varied modalities, can be employed in a [[grafo/termos/wide range|wide range]] of experimental paradigms, providing unprecedent insights into the principles of dopaminergic control, and represent the most promising light-based therapeutic approach for spatiotemporally precise correction of dopamine-related neural functions and pathologies.
