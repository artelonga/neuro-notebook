Parkinson's disease (PD) is characterized by degeneration of the [[grafo/termos/nigrostriatal dopamine|nigrostriatal dopamine]] system, resulting in progressive motor and [[grafo/termos/non-motor symptoms|nonmotor symptoms]]. Although most studies have focused on the [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia|basal ganglia]] network, recent evidence suggests that the [[grafo/termos/zona incerta|zona incerta]] (ZI), a subthalamic structure composed of 4 neurochemically defined regions, is emerging as a [[grafo/termos/therapeutic target|therapeutic target]] in PD. This review summarizes the clinical and animal studies that indicate the importance of ZI in PD. Human clinical studies have shown that subthalamotomy or [[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|deep brain stimulation]] (DBS) of the ZI alleviates muscle rigidity, [[grafo/termos/bradykinesia|bradykinesia]], [[grafo/termos/tremor|tremors]] and speech dysfunction in patients with PD. Researchers have also studied the impact of DBS of the ZI on nonmotor signs such as pain, [[grafo/termos/anxiety|anxiety]], and [[grafo/termos/depression|depression]]. Animal studies combining optogenetics, [[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|chemogenetics]], behavioral assays, and [[grafo/termos/neural activity|neural activity]] recordings reveal the functional roles of ZI GABAergic and [[grafo/termos/glutamatergic neurons|glutamatergic neurons]] in [[grafo/termos/locomotion|locomotion]], gait, and coordination of the symptoms of PD, all of which are discussed in this review. Controversies and possible future studies are also discussed.
