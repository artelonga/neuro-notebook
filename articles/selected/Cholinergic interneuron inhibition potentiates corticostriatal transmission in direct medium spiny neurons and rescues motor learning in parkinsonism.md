[[grafo/termos/striatal cholinergic interneurons|Striatal cholinergic interneurons]] (CINs) respond to salient or [[grafo/termos/reward|reward]] prediction-related stimuli after conditioning with brief pauses in their activity, implicating them in learning and [[grafo/termos/action selection|action selection]]. This pause is lost in animal models of Parkinson's disease. How this signal regulates the striatal network remains an open question. Here, we examine the impact of CIN firing inhibition on [[grafo/termos/glutamatergic transmission|glutamatergic transmission]] between the cortex and the [[grafo/termos/medium spiny neurons|medium spiny neurons]] expressing [[grafo/termos/dopamine d1 receptor|dopamine D1 receptor]] (D1 MSNs). Brief interruption of CIN activity has no effect in control conditions, whereas it increases glutamatergic responses in D1 MSNs after [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] denervation. This potentiation depends upon M4 [[grafo/termos/muscarinic receptors|muscarinic receptor]] and [[grafo/termos/protein kinase|protein kinase]] A. Decreasing CIN firing by optogenetics/[[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|chemogenetics]] in vivo partially rescues [[grafo/termos/long-term potentiation|long-term potentiation]] in MSNs and [[grafo/termos/motor learning|motor learning]] deficits in parkinsonian mice. Our findings demonstrate that the control exerted by CINs on corticostriatal transmission and striatal-dependent motor-skill learning depends on the integrity of dopaminergic inputs.
