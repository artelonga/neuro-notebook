Recent advancements in constructing human neural cells in vitro have unveiled significant therapeutic potential for treating neurological injuries and neurodegenerative diseases, such as traumatic [[grafo/termos/brain injury|brain injury]], [[grafo/termos/stroke|stroke]], Parkinson’s disease, and Alzheimer’s disease. The pivotal role of [[grafo/termos/stem cells|stem cell]] technologies, particularly [[grafo/termos/induced pluripotent stem cells|induced pluripotent stem cells]] (iPSCs) and [[grafo/termos/neural stem cells|neural stem cells]] (NSCs), is emphasized not only in modeling these disorders but also in facilitating cellular replacement therapies. The integration of [[grafo/termos/gene editing|gene editing]] techniques, such as CRISPR-Cas9, along with innovations in tissue engineering, substantially enhances cell survival and [[grafo/termos/functional integration|functional integration]] post-transplantation. Concurrently, emerging technologies like optogenetics and [[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|chemogenetics]] offer [[grafo/termos/precise control|precise control]] over cell behaviors, further promoting effective [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuit]] reconstruction. Although preclinical studies have demonstrated promising results, challenges including immune rejection, cellular heterogeneity, and long-term viability remain significant barriers to [[grafo/termos/clinical translation|clinical translation]]. A comprehensive bibliometric analysis reveals a marked increase in research interest in this field, underscoring the urgent need for sustained, rigorous investigation to optimize transplantation protocols and enhance therapeutic efficacy.
