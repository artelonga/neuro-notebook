Optogenetics has been harnessed to shed new mechanistic light on current and [[grafo/termos/future therapeutic|future therapeutic]] strategies. This has been to date achieved by the regulation of ion flow and electrical signals in [[grafo/termos/neuronal cell|neuronal cells]] and [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuits]] that are known to be affected by disease. In contrast, the optogenetic delivery of trophic biochemical signals, which support cell survival and are implicated in degenerative disorders, has never been demonstrated in an animal model of disease. Here, we reengineered the human and [[grafo/termos/drosophila melanogaster|Drosophila melanogaster]] REarranged during Transfection (hRET and dRET) receptors to be activated by light, creating one-component optogenetic tools termed Opto-hRET and Opto-dRET. Upon [[grafo/termos/blue light|blue light]] stimulation, these receptors robustly induced the MAPK/ERK proliferative [[grafo/termos/signaling pathways|signaling pathway]] in cultured cells. In PINK1B9 flies that exhibit loss of PTEN-induced putative kinase 1 (PINK1), a kinase associated with familial Parkinson's disease (PD), [[grafo/termos/light activation|light activation]] of Opto-dRET suppressed mitochondrial defects, tissue degeneration and [[grafo/termos/behavioral deficits|behavioral deficits]]. In human cells with PINK1 loss-of-function, mitochondrial fragmentation was rescued using Opto-dRET via the PI3K/NF-кB pathway. Our results demonstrate that a light-activated receptor can ameliorate disease hallmarks in a genetic model of PD. The optogenetic delivery of trophic signals is [[grafo/termos/cell-type specific|cell type-specific]] and reversible and thus has the potential to inspire novel strategies towards a spatio-temporal regulation of tissue repair.
