Optogenetics approach is used widely in neurobiology as it allows control of cellular activity with high spatial and [[grafo/termos/temporal resolution|temporal resolution]]. In most studies, optogenetics is used to control [[grafo/termos/neuronal activity|neuronal activity]]. In the present study optogenetics was used to stimulate [[grafo/termos/astrocytes|astrocytes]] with the aim to modulate neuronal activity. To achieve this goal, [[grafo/termos/light stimulation|light stimulation]] was applied to [[grafo/termos/astrocytes expressing|astrocytes expressing]] a version of [[grafo/termos/channelrhodopsin-2|ChR2]] (ionotropic opsin) or Opto-α1AR (metabotropic opsin). Optimal optogenetic [[grafo/termos/stimulation parameters|stimulation parameters]] were determined using patch-clamp recordings of hippocampal [[grafo/termos/pyramidal neurons|pyramidal neurons]]' spontaneous activity in [[grafo/termos/brain slices|brain slices]] as a readout. It was determined that the greatest increase in the number of spontaneous synaptic currents was observed when astrocytes expressing ChR2(H134R) were activated by 5 s of continuous light. For the astrocytes expressing Opto-α1AR, the greatest response was observed in the pulse stimulation mode (T = 1 s, t = 100 ms). It was also observed that activation of the astrocytic Opto-a1AR but not ChR2 results in an increase of the fEPSP slope in [[grafo/termos/hippocampal neurons|hippocampal neurons]]. Based on these results, we concluded that Opto-a1AR expressed in hippocampal astrocytes provides an opportunity to modulate the long-term [[grafo/termos/synaptic plasticity|synaptic plasticity]] optogenetically, and may potentially be used to normalize the [[grafo/termos/synaptic transmission|synaptic transmission]] and plasticity defects in a variety of neuropathological conditions, including models of Alzheimer's disease and other [[grafo/termos/neurodegenerative disorders|neurodegenerative disorders]].
