While [[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|deep brain stimulation]] (DBS) is widely employed for managing [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptoms]] in Parkinson's disease (PD), its exact [[grafo/termos/circuit mechanisms|circuit mechanisms]] remain controversial. To identify the neural targets affected by therapeutic DBS in PD, we analyzed DBS-evoked whole [[grafo/termos/brain activity|brain activity]] in female hemi-parkinsonian rats using function [[grafo/termos/magnetic resonance imaging|magnetic resonance imaging]] (fMRI). We delivered [[grafo/termos/subthalamic nucleus|subthalamic nucleus]] (STN) DBS at various stimulation pulse repetition rates using optogenetics, allowing unbiased examinations of [[grafo/termos/cell-type specific|cell-type specific]] STN feed-forward [[grafo/termos/neural activity|neural activity]]. Unilateral STN optogenetic stimulation elicited pulse repetition rate-dependent alterations of blood-oxygenation-level-dependent (BOLD) signals in [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars reticulata|SNr]] (substantia nigra pars reticulata), GP ([[grafo/termos/globus pallidus|globus pallidus]]), and CPu (caudate putamen). Notably, these manipulations effectively ameliorated pathological circling behavior in animals expressing the kinetically faster Chronos opsin, but not in animals [[grafo/termos/expressing chr2|expressing ChR2]]. Furthermore, mediation analysis revealed that the pulse repetition rate-dependent behavioral rescue was significantly mediated by optogenetically induced activity changes in GP and CPu, but not in SNr. This suggests that the activation of GP and CPu are critically involved in the [[grafo/termos/therapeutic mechanisms|therapeutic mechanisms]] of STN DBS.
