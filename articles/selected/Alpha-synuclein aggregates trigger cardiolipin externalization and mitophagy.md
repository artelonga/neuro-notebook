Accumulation of [[grafo/termos/lewy bodies|Lewy bodies]] in [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]] is associated to Parkinson disease (PD). The main component of Lewy bodies appears to be aggregates of [[grafo/termos/alpha-synuclein|alpha-synuclein]] (α-syn). Several mutations of the gene encoding this protein promote its aggregation. Thus, clustering of α-syn is considered a central event in the onset of PD. An old theory also postulates that [[grafo/termos/mitochondrial dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]] represents another cause of PD pathogenesis. However, the impact of α-syn aggregates on mitochondria remains [[grafo/termos/poorly understood|poorly understood]] considering the technical difficulties to discriminate between the different forms of α-syn. In this punctum, we describe our recent work in which we used a newly developed optogenetic tool to control the aggregation of α-syn and examine the impact on mitochondria. This work revealed that α-syn aggregates dynamically interact with mitochondria, triggering their depolarization and leading to cardiolipin translocation to the surface of mitochondria and mitophagy.
