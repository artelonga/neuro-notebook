Alzheimer's disease (AD) patients and AD mouse models usually have [[grafo/termos/cognitive impairment|cognitive impairments]], such as significant [[grafo/termos/spatial memory|spatial memory]] deficits, which are coupled with dysregulation in the structure and function of [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuits]]. The medial mammillary body (MM) is associated with spatial memory, and its dysfunction emerges in early AD. However, the link between spatial memory deficits and MM dysfunction [[grafo/termos/remains unclear|remains unclear]]. Here, by combining c-Fos mapping with optogenetic stimulation, we found the MM was involved in the retrieval of object-location [[grafo/termos/memory|memory]]. Furthermore, using viral tracing, we demonstrated the axonopathy of MM neurons in the [[grafo/termos/thalamus|thalamus]] in the [[grafo/termos/early stages|early stages]] of AD mouse model. [[grafo/termos/fiber photometry|Fiber photometry]] revealed that [[grafo/termos/neuronal activity|neuronal activity]] of MM neurons showed distinct responses to objects in different locations. However, neuronal activity was significantly reduced during the object-location [[grafo/termos/memory retrieval|memory retrieval]] phase in AD mice. Activating MM neurons in AD mice through optogenetic manipulation rescues the object-location [[grafo/termos/memory impairment|memory impairment]]. Together, our results highlight the importance of MM dysfunction in AD, which may serve as a foundation for further exploration of new targets in AD.
