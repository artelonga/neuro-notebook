The [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars reticulata|substantia nigra pars reticulata]] (SNr), a crucial [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia output|basal ganglia output]] nucleus, contains a dense expression of [[grafo/termos/dopamine d1 receptor|dopamine D1 receptors]] (D1Rs), along with dendrites belonging to [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]] of [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars compacta|substantia nigra pars compacta]]. These D1Rs are primarily located on the terminals of striatonigral [[grafo/termos/medium spiny neurons|medium spiny neurons]], suggesting their involvement in the regulation of neurotransmitter release from the [[grafo/termos/direct pathway|direct pathway]] in response to somatodendritic [[grafo/termos/dopamine release|dopamine release]]. To explore the hypothesis that D1Rs modulate [[grafo/termos/gaba release|GABA release]] from striatonigral [[grafo/termos/synapse|synapses]], we conducted optical recordings of striatonigral activity and postsynaptic patch-clamp recordings from SNr neurons in the presence of [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] and D1R agonists. We found that dopamine inhibits optogenetically triggered striatonigral GABA release by modulating vesicle fusion and Ca 2+ influx in striatonigral boutons. Notably, the effect of DA was independent of D1R activity but required activation of 5-HT1B receptors. Our results suggest a serotonergic mechanism involved in the therapeutic actions of dopaminergic medications for Parkinson's disease and psychostimulant-related disorders.
