Significance Chronic stress has emerged in the epidemiologic literature as a [[grafo/termos/risk factor|risk factor]] for both psychiatric and neurodegenerative diseases. Thus, neurologic maladaptation to chronic stress is highly relevant to the pathogenesis of human diseases such as [[grafo/termos/depression|depression]] and Alzheimer's disease, yet it remains [[grafo/termos/poorly understood|poorly understood]]. Here we report a study of the [[grafo/termos/neural circuits|neural circuits]] and [[grafo/termos/molecular pathways|molecular pathways]] that govern the relationship between stress and [[grafo/termos/cognition|cognition]]. We present data demonstrating that behavioral stress impairs [[grafo/termos/cognitive function|cognitive function]] via activation of a specific direct neural circuit from the [[grafo/termos/basolateral amygdala|basolateral amygdala]] to the dorsal [[grafo/termos/hippocampus|hippocampus]]. Moreover, we delineate a [[grafo/termos/molecular mechanisms|molecular mechanism]] by which behavioral stress is translated to hippocampal dysfunction via a p25/Cdk5 (cyclin-dependent kinase 5)-dependent pathway and epigenetic alterations of neuroplasticity-related [[grafo/termos/gene expression|gene expression]]. Repeated stress has been suggested to underlie learning and [[grafo/termos/memory deficits|memory deficits]] via the basolateral amygdala (BLA) and the hippocampus; however, the functional contribution of BLA inputs to the hippocampus and their molecular repercussions are not well understood. Here we show that repeated stress is accompanied by generation of the Cdk5 (cyclin-dependent kinase 5)-activator p25, up-regulation and phosphorylation of glucocorticoid receptors, increased HDAC2 expression, and reduced expression of memory-related genes in the hippocampus. A combination of optogenetic and pharmacosynthetic approaches shows that BLA activation is both necessary and sufficient for stress-associated molecular changes and [[grafo/termos/memory impairment|memory impairments]]. Furthermore, we show that this effect relies on direct glutamatergic projections from the BLA to the dorsal hippocampus. Finally, we show that p25 generation is necessary for the stress-induced [[grafo/termos/memory|memory]] dysfunction. Taken together, our data provide a neural circuit model for stress-induced [[grafo/termos/hippocampal memory|hippocampal memory]] deficits through BLA activity-dependent p25 generation.
