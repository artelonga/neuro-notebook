BACKGROUND: Long-term [[grafo/termos/levodopa|levodopa]] ([[grafo/termos/l-dopa|l-dopa]]) treatment is associated with the development of [[grafo/termos/l-dopa-induced dyskinesia|l-dopa-induced dyskinesias]] in the majority of patients with Parkinson disease (PD). The etiopathogonesis and [[grafo/termos/mechanisms underlying|mechanisms underlying]] l-dopa-induced dyskinesias are not well understood. METHODS: We used striatal optogenetic stimulation to induce [[grafo/termos/dyskinesia|dyskinesias]] in a hemiparkinsonian model of PD in rats. [[grafo/termos/striatal dopamine depletion|Striatal dopamine depletion]] was induced unilaterally by [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-hydroxydopamine]] injection into the medial forebrain bundle. For the optogenetic manipulation, we injected [[grafo/termos/adeno-associated virus|adeno-associated virus]] particles expressing [[grafo/termos/channelrhodopsin|channelrhodopsin]] to stimulate [[grafo/termos/striatal medium spiny neurons|striatal medium spiny neurons]] with a laser source. RESULTS: Simultaneous optical activation of [[grafo/termos/medium spiny neurons|medium spiny neurons]] of the direct and indirect striatal pathways in the [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine lesion|6-hydroxydopamine lesion]] but l-dopa naïve rats induced [[grafo/termos/involuntary movements|involuntary movements]] similar to l-dopa-induced dyskinesias, labeled here as optodyskinesias. Noticeably, optodyskinesias were facilitated by l-dopa in animals that did not respond initially to the laser stimulation. In general, optodyskinesias lasted while the laser stimulus was applied, but in some instances remained ongoing for a few seconds after the laser was off. Postmortem tissue analysis revealed increased FosB expression, a molecular marker of l-dopa-induced dyskinesias, primarily in medium spiny neurons of the [[grafo/termos/direct pathway|direct pathway]] in the dopamine-depleted hemisphere. CONCLUSION: Selective optogenetic activation of the [[grafo/termos/dorsolateral striatum|dorsolateral striatum]] elicits dyskinesias in the 6-hydroxydopamine [[grafo/termos/rat model|rat model]] of PD. This effect was associated with a preferential activation of the direct striato-nigral pathway. These results potentially open new avenues in the understanding of [[grafo/termos/mechanisms involved|mechanisms involved]] in l-dopa-induced dyskinesias. © 2017 International Parkinson and [[grafo/termos/movement disorders|Movement Disorder]] Society.
