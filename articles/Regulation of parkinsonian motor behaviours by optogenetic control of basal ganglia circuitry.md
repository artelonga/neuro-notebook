[[grafo/termos/neural circuits|Neural circuits]] of the [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia|basal ganglia]] are critical for motor planning and [[grafo/termos/action selection|action selection]]. Two parallel basal ganglia pathways have been described, and have been proposed to exert opposing influences on [[grafo/termos/motor function|motor function]]. According to this classical model, activation of the 'direct' pathway facilitates movement and activation of the 'indirect' pathway inhibits movement. However, more recent anatomical and functional evidence has called into question the validity of this hypothesis. Because this model has never been empirically tested, the specific function of these circuits in [[grafo/termos/behaving animals|behaving animals]] remains unknown. Here we report direct activation of [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia circuitry|basal ganglia circuitry]] in vivo, using optogenetic control of [[grafo/termos/direct and indirect pathways|direct- and indirect-pathway]] medium [[grafo/termos/spiny projection neurons|spiny projection neurons]] ([[grafo/termos/medium spiny neurons|MSNs]]), achieved through Cre-dependent viral expression of [[grafo/termos/channelrhodopsin-2|channelrhodopsin-2]] in the [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]] of bacterial artificial chromosome transgenic mice expressing Cre recombinase under control of regulatory elements for the [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] D1 or D2 receptor. Bilateral excitation of [[grafo/termos/indirect pathway|indirect-pathway]] MSNs elicited a [[grafo/termos/parkinsonian states|parkinsonian state]], distinguished by increased freezing, [[grafo/termos/bradykinesia|bradykinesia]] and decreased locomotor initiations. In contrast, activation of [[grafo/termos/direct pathway|direct-pathway]] MSNs reduced freezing and increased [[grafo/termos/locomotion|locomotion]]. In a mouse model of Parkinson's disease, direct-pathway activation completely rescued deficits in freezing, bradykinesia and locomotor initiation. Taken together, our findings establish a critical role for basal ganglia circuitry in the bidirectional regulation of motor behaviour and indicate that modulation of direct-pathway circuitry may represent an [[grafo/termos/effective therapeutic|effective therapeutic]] strategy for [[grafo/termos/ameliorating parkinsonian motor|ameliorating parkinsonian motor]] deficits.
