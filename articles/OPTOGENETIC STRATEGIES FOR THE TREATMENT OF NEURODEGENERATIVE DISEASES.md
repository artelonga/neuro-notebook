Optogenetic tools and strategies, as an innovative approach to control neurodegenerative diseases, have been developed in recent years with the application of a combination of optical and genetic techniques on a certain group of cells of [[grafo/termos/living tissues|living tissue]]. With this technique, a particular [[grafo/termos/cell types|cell type]] and their predefined pathway can be controlled precisely. The basic steps of this optogenetics include discovery and placing of [[grafo/termos/light sensitive|light-sensitive]] molecules into cells to provide [[grafo/termos/optical control|optical control]] and applications in animal experiments. Strategies for suppressing neurons are based on activation of light-sensitive prokaryotic membrane proteins that act as [[grafo/termos/ion channels|ion channels]] and transporters. Optogenetic strategies that are adapted clinically have the capacity to provide temporal, regional and cellular specific delivery in ways that no other treatment may suggest. Engineering [[grafo/termos/channelrhodopsin|channelrhodopsin]](CR) molecule and its expression are very important in terms of optogenetic experiments. Because experiments conducted with conventional CR are not suitable for testing chronic long-term studies, there is a certain need for a controlled medium for optogenetic neurodegenerative disease experiments. Keywords: Alzheimer’s disease; neurodegenerative diseases; optogenetics; Parkinson disease
