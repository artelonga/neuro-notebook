Early [[grafo/termos/non-motor symptoms|non-motor symptoms]] such as [[grafo/termos/mood disorders|mood disorders]] and [[grafo/termos/cognitive deficits|cognitive deficits]] are increasingly recognised in Parkinson's disease (PD). They may precede the characteristic motor symptomatology caused by [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] (DA) [[grafo/termos/neuronal loss|neuronal loss]] in the [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra pars compacta|substantia nigra pars compacta]] (SNc). It is well known that [[grafo/termos/striatal cholinergic interneurons|striatal cholinergic interneurons]] (ChIs) are emerging as key regulators of PD [[grafo/termos/motor symptoms|motor symptom]], however, their involvement in the cognitive and affective alterations occurring in the premotor phase of PD is [[grafo/termos/poorly understood|poorly understood]]. We used optogenetic photoinhibition of striatal ChIs in mice with mild nigrostriatal [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-hydroxydopamine]] (6-OHDA) lesions and assessed their role in anxiety-like behaviour in the elevated plus maze, [[grafo/termos/social memory|social memory]] recognition of a congener and visuospatial object recognition. In transgenic mice specifically expressing [[grafo/termos/halorhodopsin|halorhodopsin]] (eNpHR) in [[grafo/termos/cholinergic neurons|cholinergic neurons]], striatal ChIs photoinhibition reduced the anxiety-like behaviour and reversed social and spatial short-term [[grafo/termos/memory impairment|memory impairment]] induced by moderate DA depletion (e.g., 50% loss of [[grafo/termos/tyrosine hydroxylase|tyrosine hydroxylase]] TH-positive neurons in the SNc). Systemic injection of telenzepine (0.3 mg/kg), a preferential M1 muscarinic [[grafo/termos/cholinergic receptors|cholinergic receptors]] antagonist, improved anxiety-like behaviour, social memory recognition but not [[grafo/termos/spatial memory|spatial memory]] deficits. Our results suggest that dysfunction of the [[grafo/termos/striatal cholinergic|striatal cholinergic]] system may play a role in the short-term cognitive and emotional deficits of partially DA-depleted mice. Blocking cholinergic activity with M1 [[grafo/termos/muscarinic receptors|muscarinic receptor]] antagonists may represent a possible [[grafo/termos/therapeutic target|therapeutic target]], although not exclusive, to modulate these early non-motor deficits.
