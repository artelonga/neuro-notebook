Optogenetics is a transformational technology in neuroscience that allows the control of neural circuits in real time in awake, behaving animals and thus begins the era of causal neuroscience: making it possible to manipulate the brain and record an animal’s response rather than only observing and making inferences. Optogenetics can reproducibly turn on and turn off neural function with cell-specific, millisecond precision commensurate with how the brain works. The new technology includes the insertion into cells of genes that bestow light responsiveness to the proteins they produce (notably, Channelrhodopsins), the means for delivering light deep into living, freely moving animals, the targeting of light-sensitivity only to cells of interest, and ways for evaluating the effects of this optical control on behavior. Optogenetic studies have contributed to our understanding of psychiatric illnesses such as anxiety, depression, addiction, and autism as well as neurodegenerative disease in the form of Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s. Optogenetic research has also revealed a new understanding of normal brain functions. What sets sleep/wake cycles? How do we manage to stay hydrated? Is parental behavior somehow innate? To both genders? What are the mechanisms behind memory formation, storage, and retrieval? How is perception encoded in the brain? Other radical twenty-first century contributions to neurotechnology include tissue clearing—the ability to make brain tissue transparent to see brainwide wiring of individual neurons—and cerebral organoids—self-organizing clumps of brain tissue used to decipher neural development and neurodevelopmental diseases.
