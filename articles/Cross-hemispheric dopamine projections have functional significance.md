[[grafo/termos/dopamine signaling|Dopamine signaling]] occurs on a subsecond timescale, and its dysregulation is implicated in pathologies ranging from [[grafo/termos/drug addiction|drug addiction]] to Parkinson's disease. Anatomic evidence suggests that some [[grafo/termos/dopamine neurons|dopamine neurons]] have cross-hemispheric projections, but the significance of these projections is unknown. Here we report unprecedented interhemispheric communication in the [[grafo/termos/midbrain dopamine|midbrain dopamine]] system of awake and anesthetized rats. In the anesthetized rats, optogenetic and [[grafo/termos/electrical stimulation|electrical stimulation]] of [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] cells elicited physiologically relevant [[grafo/termos/dopamine release|dopamine release]] in the contralateral [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]]. Contralateral release differed between the dorsal and [[grafo/termos/ventral striatum|ventral striatum]] owing to differential regulation by D2-like receptors. In the [[grafo/termos/freely moving|freely moving]] animals, simultaneous bilateral measurements revealed that dopamine release synchronizes between hemispheres and intact, contralateral projections can release dopamine in the midbrain of 6-hydroxydopamine-lesioned rats. These experiments are the first, to our knowledge, to show cross-hemispheric synchronicity in dopamine signaling and support a functional role for contralateral projections. In addition, our data reveal that psychostimulants, such as amphetamine, promote the coupling of dopamine transients between hemispheres.
