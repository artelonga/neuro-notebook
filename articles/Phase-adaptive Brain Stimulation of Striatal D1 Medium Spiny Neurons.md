[[grafo/termos/brain rhythms|Brain rhythms]] are strongly linked with behavior, and abnormal rhythms can signify pathophysiology. For instance, the [[grafo/termos/basal ganglia|basal ganglia]] exhibit a [[grafo/termos/wide range|wide range]] of low-frequency oscillations during movement, but pathological “beta” rhythms at ~ 20 Hz have been observed in Parkinson’s disease (PD) and in PD animal models. All brain rhythms have a frequency, which describes how often they oscillate, and a phase, which describes the precise time that peaks and troughs of brain rhythms occur. Although frequency has been extensively studied, the relevance of phase is unknown, in part because it is difficult to causally manipulate the instantaneous phase of ongoing brain rhythms. Here, we developed a phase-adaptive, real-time, closed-loop algorithm to deliver optogenetic stimulation at a specific phase with millisecond latency. We combined this P hase- A daptive B rain ST imulation (PABST) approach with [[grafo/termos/cell-type specific|cell-type-specific]] optogenetic methods to stimulate basal ganglia networks in dopamine-depleted mice that model motor aspects of human PD. We focused on [[grafo/termos/striatal medium spiny neurons|striatal medium spiny neurons]] expressing D1-type [[grafo/termos/dopamine receptors|dopamine receptors]] because these neurons can facilitate movement. We report three main results. First, we found that our approach delivered PABST within system latencies of 13 milliseconds. Second, we report that [[grafo/termos/closed-loop stimulation|closed-loop stimulation]] powerfully influenced the spike-field coherence of local brain rhythms within the [[grafo/termos/dorsal striatum|dorsal striatum]]. Finally, we found that 4-Hz, but not 20-Hz, PABST improved movement speed by 41%. These data provide causal evidence that phase is relevant for [[grafo/termos/brain stimulation|brain stimulation]], which will allow for more precise, targeted, and individualized brain stimulation. Our findings are applicable to a broad range of preclinical brain stimulation approaches and could also inform circuit-specific [[grafo/termos/neuromodulation|neuromodulation]] treatments for [[grafo/termos/human brain|human brain]] disease.
