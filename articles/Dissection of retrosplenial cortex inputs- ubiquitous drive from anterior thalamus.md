The [[grafo/termos/retrosplenial cortex|retrosplenial cortex]] (RSC) is a highly interconnected [[grafo/termos/brain regions|brain region]] involved in [[grafo/termos/spatial navigation|spatial navigation]] and associative learning. It forms extensive, reciprocal connections with sensory, hippocampal, parahippocampal, prefrontal, and thalamic areas. RSC comprises granular (gRSC) and dysgranular (dRSC) subdivisions with distinct connectivity and functions. Despite its emerging role in behaviour and its implication in memory-related disorders such as Alzheimer’s disease, the nature of its synaptic inputs remains [[grafo/termos/poorly understood|poorly understood]]. Here, we combined viral anatomical tracing, optogenetic stimulation, and patch-clamp [[grafo/termos/electrophysiology|electrophysiology]] to investigate inputs from the [[grafo/termos/anterior cingulate cortex|anterior cingulate cortex]] (ACC), dorsal subiculum (dSub), and anterior thalamic nuclei (ATN) to gRSC and dRSC. Strikingly, all recorded RSC [[grafo/termos/pyramidal neurons|pyramidal neurons]] received ATN input, regardless of subdivision or cortical layer. Activation of ATN inputs evoked significantly larger post-synaptic responses than those from dSub or ACC, though both regions maintained substantial connectivity with RSC. While dSub projections appeared denser in gRSC, synaptic responses were larger in dRSC, albeit with lower input probability. Notably, NMDA receptor-mediated components of RSC excitatory inputs were weaker than expected, potentially explaining the reported inability to induce [[grafo/termos/long-term potentiation|long-term potentiation]] in RSC in ex vivo neurophysiology experiments. This is the first study to characterise the [[grafo/termos/synaptic properties|synaptic properties]] of retrosplenial afferents. Our findings highlight the dominant influence of ATN inputs and raise important questions about how RSC’s long-range connectivity supports its roles in [[grafo/termos/memory|memory]] and spatial navigation.
