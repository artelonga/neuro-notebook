The [[grafo/termos/cerebellum|cerebellum]] is best known for its role in controlling [[grafo/termos/motor behaviors|motor behaviors]]. However, recent work supports the view that it also influences non-motor behaviors. The contribution of the cerebellum towards different [[grafo/termos/brain function|brain functions]] is underscored by its involvement in a diverse and increasing number of neurological and neuropsychiatric conditions including ataxia, [[grafo/termos/dystonia|dystonia]], [[grafo/termos/essential tremor|essential tremor]], Parkinson's disease (PD), [[grafo/termos/epilepsy|epilepsy]], [[grafo/termos/stroke|stroke]], [[grafo/termos/multiple sclerosis|multiple sclerosis]], [[grafo/termos/autism spectrum disorder|autism spectrum disorders]], dyslexia, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), and schizophrenia. Although there are no cures for these conditions, cerebellar stimulation is quickly gaining attention for symptomatic alleviation, as cerebellar circuitry has arisen as a promising target for invasive and [[grafo/termos/non-invasive neuromodulation|non-invasive neuromodulation]]. This consensus paper brings together experts from the fields of neurophysiology, neurology, and neurosurgery to discuss recent efforts in using the cerebellum as a [[grafo/termos/therapeutic interventions|therapeutic intervention]]. We report on the most advanced techniques for manipulating cerebellar circuits in humans and animal models and define key hurdles and questions for moving forward.
