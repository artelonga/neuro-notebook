As life span increases, neurodegenerative diseases such as dementia, Parkinson's disease, Huntington's disease, [[grafo/termos/amyotrophic lateral sclerosis|amyotrophic lateral sclerosis]] become an emerging problem in modern society. In particular Alzheimer's disease (AD), characterized by a progressive [[grafo/termos/cognitive impairment|cognitive impairment]] and [[grafo/termos/memory loss|memory loss]], is the dominant cause of disability in people aged over 60. Due to the lack of accurate models, understanding the disease mechanisms and developing a cure for AD remains challenging. However, a novel approach based on human [[grafo/termos/induced pluripotent stem cells|induced pluripotent stem cell]] (iPSC) technology may offer an opportunity to overcome the limitations of the current models. These cells obtained by reprogramming patient's somatic cells such as fibroblasts can be differentiated in vitro into various types of neural cells which further develop complex networks. To explore these heterogeneous [[grafo/termos/neural network|neural networks]], it is often critical to understand the activity of multiple neurons and how they communicate with each other.
