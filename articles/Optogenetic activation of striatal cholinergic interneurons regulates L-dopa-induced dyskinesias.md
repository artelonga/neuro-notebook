[[grafo/termos/l-dopa-induced dyskinesia|L-dopa-induced dyskinesias]] ([[grafo/termos/levodopa-induced dyskinesia|LIDs]]) are a serious complication of [[grafo/termos/l-dopa|L-dopa]] therapy for Parkinson's disease. Emerging [[grafo/termos/evidence indicates|evidence indicates]] that the nicotinic cholinergic system plays a role in LIDs, although the pathways and mechanisms are [[grafo/termos/poorly understood|poorly understood]]. Here we used optogenetics to investigate the role of [[grafo/termos/striatal cholinergic interneurons|striatal cholinergic interneurons]] in LIDs. Mice expressing cre-recombinase under the control of the choline acetyltransferase promoter (ChAT-Cre) were lesioned by unilateral injection of [[grafo/termos/6-hydroxydopamine|6-hydroxydopamine]]. AAV5-ChR2-eYFP or AAV5-control-eYFP was injected into the [[grafo/termos/dorsolateral striatum|dorsolateral striatum]], and [[grafo/termos/optical fibers|optical fibers]] implanted. After stable virus expression, mice were treated with L-dopa. They were then subjected to various stimulation protocols for 2h and LIDs rated. Continuous stimulation with a short duration optical pulse (1-5ms) enhanced LIDs. This effect was blocked by the general [[grafo/termos/muscarinic acetylcholine receptor|muscarinic acetylcholine receptor]] (mAChR) antagonist atropine indicating it was mAChR-mediated. By contrast, continuous stimulation with a longer duration optical pulse (20ms to 1s) reduced LIDs to a similar extent as nicotine treatment (~50%). The general [[grafo/termos/nicotinic acetylcholine receptors|nicotinic acetylcholine receptor]] (nAChR) antagonist mecamylamine blocked the decline in LIDs with longer optical pulses showing it was nAChR-mediated. None of the stimulation regimens altered LIDs in control-eYFP mice. Lesion-induced [[grafo/termos/motor impairment|motor impairment]] was not affected by [[grafo/termos/optical stimulation|optical stimulation]] indicating that cholinergic transmission selectively regulates LIDs. Longer pulse stimulation increased the number of c-Fos expressing ChAT neurons, suggesting that changes in this immediate early gene may be involved. These results demonstrate that striatal cholinergic interneurons play a critical role in LIDs and support the idea that nicotine treatment reduces LIDs via nAChR desensitization.
