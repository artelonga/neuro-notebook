[[grafo/termos/dopamine signaling|Dopamine signaling]] disruptions are linked to a range of disorders, including Parkinson&rsquo;s disease and schizophrenia, which often manifest [[grafo/termos/sleep disturbances|sleep disturbances]] as a common symptom. This study explored the function of [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]] in managing [[grafo/termos/sleep-wake cycles|sleep-wake cycles]] in fruit flies ([[grafo/termos/drosophila melanogaster|Drosophila melanogaster]]), utilizing optogenetic techniques to selectively activate or deactivate these neurons. The impact on [[grafo/termos/sleep|sleep]] and [[grafo/termos/activity patterns|activity patterns]] was assessed using the Drosophila Activity Monitoring System. Our results demonstrate that prolonged deactivation of dopaminergic neurons significantly reduced nocturnal sleep in fruit flies, whereas prolonged activation not only decreased diurnal sleep but also led to more fragmented sleep patterns. These findings underscore the critical role of dopaminergic neurons in sleep regulation and suggest that manipulating these neurons in fruit flies can mimic sleep-wake disturbances observed in human diseases affecting [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] pathways. These insights lay the groundwork for deeper investigation into how disease-specific factors influence sleep-wake cycles and the [[grafo/termos/underlying mechanisms|underlying mechanisms]] of sleep disruption in conditions like Parkinson&rsquo;s disease and schizophrenia.
