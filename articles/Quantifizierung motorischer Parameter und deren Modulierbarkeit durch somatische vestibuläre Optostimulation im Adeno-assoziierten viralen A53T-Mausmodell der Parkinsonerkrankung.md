Postural instability and gait difficulty (PIGD) represents a prevalent and progressive clinical problem in Parkinson’s disease (PD), associated with tremendous influence on quality of life, morbidity, mortality and hospitalization burden. This phenotype lacks a clear biological basis and is unsatisfactorily treated by routine care with dopaminergic medication and subthalamic nucleus deep brain stimulation (STN-DBS). Hence, PIGD urgently requires elucidation of pathocircuitry, plus novel or refined pharmacotherapeutic and/or neuromodulatory treatment approaches. Non-invasive neuromodulation of the vestibular system, particularly the vestibular nucleus complex (VNC), represents a promising therapeutic strategy, yet the involved circuitry is still poorly understood. In this work, we show that the mouse VNC provides widespread Vglut2-positive projections to striato–thalamo–subthalamic and caudal medullary motor centers, but not to the mesencephalic locomotor region. Optogenetic stimulation of excitatory VNC neurons at intensities just below the threshold for vestibular symptoms activated these basal ganglia–brainstem axis targets. Unsupervised analysis of pose dynamics revealed a global increase in behavioral transitions and locomotor activity, which was further supported by conventional kinematic assessments. Functionally, such perithreshold VNC stimulation resynchronized natural gait patterns and improved locomotor performance—though not maximal capacity—in parkinsonian mice. These findings highlight excitatory VNC circuit mechanisms as a potential target for therapeutic retuning of motor dysfunction in PD.
