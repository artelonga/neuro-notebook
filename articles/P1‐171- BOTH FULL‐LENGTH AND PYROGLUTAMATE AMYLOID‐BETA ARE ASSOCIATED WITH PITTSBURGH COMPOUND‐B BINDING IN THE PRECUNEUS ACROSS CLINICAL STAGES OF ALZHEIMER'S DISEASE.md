Given that [[grafo/termos/anxiety|anxiety]] can accelerate [[grafo/termos/memory deficits|memory deficits]] during AD process, we performed Barnes maze (BM) to test the [[grafo/termos/cognitive function|cognitive functions]] in APP/PS1 mice. Last, we investigate whether optogenetic activation of BLP–vCA1 inputs improves anxiety and memory deficit in APP/ PS1 mice. Results: APP/PS1 mice (6 m-old) showed more robust intraneuronal Ab and extraneuronal amyloid plaques in BLP than BLA detected by 6E10 staining. Then, in wildtype mice, both cFos+ and CTB+ neurons were much greater in BLP than those in BLA, indicating stronger activation of BLP–vCA1 circuit in normal condition. The activation was barely detectable in APP/PS1 mice, suggesting that an insufficient activation of BLP–vCA1 circuit can disrupt the balance between BLA–vCA1 and BLP–vCA1; next, photoactivation of BLP–vCA1 inputs in APP/PS1 mice increased open-arm entry probability, open-arm exploration time in EPM test, center exploration time in OFT with an increased time in target and correct pokes without changing motor ability in BM. Conclusions: BLP–vCA1 inputs deficit may link anxiety and [[grafo/termos/memory impairment|memory impairment]], and, activation of BLP–vCA1 inputs may rescue anxiety associated memory deficit in AD.
