In Parkinson's disease, the degeneration of [[grafo/termos/dopaminergic neurons|dopaminergic neurons]] in [[grafo/termos/substantia nigra|substantia nigra]] leads to a decrease in the physiological levels of [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] in [[grafo/termos/striatum|striatum]]. The existing dopaminergic therapies effectively alleviate the symptoms, albeit they do not revert the disease progression and result in significant adverse effects. Transplanting dopaminergic neurons derived from [[grafo/termos/stem cells|stem cells]] could restore dopamine levels without additional [[grafo/termos/motor complications|motor complications]]. However, the transplanted cells disperse in vivo and it is not possible to stimulate them on demand to modulate [[grafo/termos/dopamine release|dopamine release]] to prevent [[grafo/termos/dyskinesia|dyskinesia]]. In order to address these issues, this [[grafo/termos/paper presents|paper presents]] a multifunctional leaky optoelectrical fiber for potential [[grafo/termos/neuromodulation|neuromodulation]] and as a cell substrate for application in combined optogenetic [[grafo/termos/stem cell therapy|stem cell therapy]]. Pyrolytic carbon coated [[grafo/termos/optical fibers|optical fibers]] are laser ablated to pattern micro-optical windows to permit light leakage over a large area. The pyrolytic carbon acts as an excellent electrode for the electrochemical detection of dopamine. Human [[grafo/termos/neural stem cells|neural stem cells]] are genetically modified to express the [[grafo/termos/light sensitive|light sensitive]] opsin [[grafo/termos/channelrhodopsin-2|channelrhodopsin-2]] and are differentiated into dopaminergic neurons on the leaky optoelectrical fiber. Finally, light leaking from the micro-optical windows is used to stimulate the dopaminergic neurons resulting in the release of dopamine that is detected in real-time using chronoamperometry.
