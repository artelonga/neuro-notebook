SUMMARY Chronic [[grafo/termos/levodopa|Levodopa]] therapy, the gold-standard treatment of Parkinson’s Disease (PD), leads to the emergence of [[grafo/termos/involuntary movements|involuntary movements]], called [[grafo/termos/levodopa-induced dyskinesia|levodopa-induced dyskinesia]] (LID). Cerebellar stimulations have been shown to decrease LID severity in PD patients. Here, in order to determine how cerebellar stimulations induce LID alleviation, we performed daily short trains of optogenetic stimulations of Purkinje cells (PC) in [[grafo/termos/freely moving|freely moving]] mice. We demonstrated that these stimulations are sufficient to suppress LID or even prevent their development. This symptomatic relief is accompanied by the normalization of aberrant neuronal discharge in the cerebellar nuclei, the [[grafo/termos/motor cortex|motor cortex]] and the parafascicular [[grafo/termos/thalamus|thalamus]]. Inhibition of the cerebello-parafascicular pathway counteracted the beneficial effect of cerebellar stimulations. Moreover, cerebellar stimulations reversed plasticity in D1 [[grafo/termos/striatal neurons|striatal neurons]] and normalized the overexpression of FosB, a [[grafo/termos/transcription factor|transcription factor]] causally linked to LID. These findings demonstrate LID alleviation and prevention by daily PC stimulations, which restore the function of a wide brain motor network, and may be valuable for LID treatment.
