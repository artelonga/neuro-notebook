Parkinson's disease (PD) is known to alter the intrinsic properties of [[grafo/termos/striatal cholinergic interneurons|striatal cholinergic interneurons]] (ChIs). However, how PD shapes ChI control of intrastriatal GABAergic circuits regulating principal [[grafo/termos/spiny projection neurons|spiny projection neurons]] (SPNs) is unknown. To fill this gap, striatal circuits in healthy and parkinsonian mice were interrogated. In ex [[grafo/termos/vivo brain|vivo brain]] slices from healthy mice, optogenetic stimulation of ChIs evoked [[grafo/termos/gabaa receptor|GABAA receptor]] currents in both indirect and [[grafo/termos/direct pathway|direct pathway]] SPNs that were attributable to [[grafo/termos/nicotinic acetylcholine receptors|nicotinic acetylcholine receptor]] (nAChR)-mediated activation of [[grafo/termos/gabaergic interneurons|GABAergic interneurons]] (GIs). Simulations suggested that this circuit exerts a state-dependent control of SPN dendritic integration that was modulated by concomitant [[grafo/termos/muscarinic receptors|muscarinic receptor]] signaling. Surprisingly, in mouse models of prodromal and [[grafo/termos/parkinsonian states|parkinsonian states]], the ability of ChIs to engage this intrastriatal circuitry was disrupted because interneurons down-regulated nicotinic AChRs. Taken together, these studies suggest that impaired ChI control of GABAergic interneurons contributes to [[grafo/termos/behavioral deficits|behavioral deficits]] in both prodromal and clinical PD states.
