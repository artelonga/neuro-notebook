The brain&s;s process of learning and memory formation is primarily driven by synaptic plasticity, which is the capacity of synapses to become stronger or weaker over time in response to activity. This phenomenon involves complex molecular and cellular pathways and encompasses both long-term depression (LTD) and long-term potentiation (LTP). The function of neurotransmitters, ion channels, and intracellular signaling cascades are highlighted in this chapter, which examines molecular and cellular mechanism driven synaptic plasticity. This chapter also explores the various uses of synaptic plasticity research. Knowing synaptic plasticity is important because it can help treat neurological disorders like multiple sclerosis, Parkinson&s;s disease, and Alzheimer&s;s disease, where impaired synapse function leads to cognitive decline. Later on in this chapter, different techniques such as optogenetics and chemogenetics through which researchers are trying to monitor these modulations in synapse functions, are also discussed. So by elucidating the intricate interplay of neurotransmitters, receptors, and signaling pathways, researchers can harness synaptic plasticity to unlock new therapeutic avenues and improve cognitive function and quality of life.
