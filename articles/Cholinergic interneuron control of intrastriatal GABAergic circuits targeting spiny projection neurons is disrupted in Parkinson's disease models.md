This Dandiset contains all raw and primary-processed data underlying the study '[[grafo/termos/cholinergic interneurons|Cholinergic interneuron]] control of intrastriatal GABAergic circuits targeting [[grafo/termos/spiny projection neurons|spiny projection neurons]] is disrupted in Parkinson’s disease models'. Parkinson’s disease (PD) is known to alter the intrinsic properties of [[grafo/termos/striatal cholinergic interneurons|striatal cholinergic interneurons]] (ChIs). However, how PD shapes ChI control of intrastriatal GABAergic circuits regulating principal spiny projection neurons (SPNs) is unknown. To fill this gap, striatal circuits in healthy and parkinsonian mice were interrogated. In ex [[grafo/termos/vivo brain|vivo brain]] slices from healthy mice, optogenetic stimulation of ChIs evoked [[grafo/termos/gabaa receptor|GABAA receptor]] currents in both indirect and [[grafo/termos/direct pathway|direct pathway]] SPNs that were attributable to [[grafo/termos/nicotinic acetylcholine receptors|nicotinic acetylcholine receptor]] (nAChR)-mediated activation of [[grafo/termos/gabaergic interneurons|GABAergic interneurons]]. Simulations suggested that this circuit exerts a state-dependent control of SPN dendritic integration that was modulated by concomitant [[grafo/termos/muscarinic receptors|muscarinic receptor]] signaling. Surprisingly, in mouse models of prodromal and [[grafo/termos/parkinsonian states|parkinsonian states]], the ability of ChIs to engage this intrastriatal circuitry was disrupted because interneurons down-regulated nicotinic AChRs. Taken together, these studies suggest that impaired ChI control of GABAergic interneurons contribute to [[grafo/termos/behavioral deficits|behavioral deficits]] in both prodromal and clinical PD states.
