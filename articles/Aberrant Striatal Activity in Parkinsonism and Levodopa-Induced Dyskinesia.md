[[grafo/termos/action selection|Action selection]] relies on the coordinated activity of striatal [[grafo/termos/direct and indirect pathways|direct and indirect pathway]] [[grafo/termos/medium spiny neurons|medium spiny neurons]] (dMSNs and iMSNs, respectively). Loss of [[grafo/termos/dopamine|dopamine]] in Parkinson's disease is thought to disrupt this balance. While [[grafo/termos/dopamine replacement|dopamine replacement]] with [[grafo/termos/levodopa|levodopa]] may restore normal function, the development of [[grafo/termos/involuntary movements|involuntary movements]] ([[grafo/termos/levodopa-induced dyskinesia|levodopa-induced dyskinesia]] [LID]) limits therapy. How chronic dopamine loss and replacement with levodopa modulate the firing of identified MSNs in [[grafo/termos/behaving animals|behaving animals]] is unknown. Using optogenetically labeled striatal single-unit recordings, we assess circuit dysfunction in [[grafo/termos/parkinsonism|parkinsonism]] and LID. Counter to current models, we found that following [[grafo/termos/dopamine depletion|dopamine depletion]], iMSN firing was elevated only during periods of immobility, while dMSN firing was dramatically and persistently reduced. Most notably, we identified a subpopulation of dMSNs with abnormally high levodopa-evoked [[grafo/termos/firing rate|firing rates]], which correlated specifically with [[grafo/termos/dyskinesia|dyskinesia]]. These findings provide key insights into the [[grafo/termos/circuit mechanisms underlying|circuit mechanisms underlying]] parkinsonism and LID, with implications for developing targeted therapies.
