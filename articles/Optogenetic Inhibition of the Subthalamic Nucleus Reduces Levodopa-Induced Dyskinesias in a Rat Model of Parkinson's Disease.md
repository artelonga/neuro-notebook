BACKGROUND: The inhibition of [[grafo/termos/neuronal activity|neuronal activity]] by electrical [[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|deep brain stimulation]] is one of the mechanisms explaining the amelioration of [[grafo/termos/levodopa-induced dyskinesia|levodopa-induced dyskinesia]]. However, electrical deep brain stimulation cannot specifically activate or inactivate selected types of neurons. OBJECTIVES: We applied optogenetics as an alternative treatment to deep brain stimulation for levodopa-induced dyskinesia, and also to confirm that the mechanism of levodopa-induced dyskinesia amelioration by [[grafo/termos/subthalamic nucleus|subthalamic nucleus]] deep brain stimulation is mediated through neuronal inhibition. METHODS: 6-hydroxydopamine-induced hemiparkinsonian rats received injections of hSynapsin1-NpHR-YFP [[grafo/termos/adeno-associated virus|adeno-associated virus]] (AAV) or hSynapsin1-YFP AAV. Two weeks after viral injections, all rats were treated with daily injections of [[grafo/termos/levodopa|levodopa]]. Then, the optic fiber was implanted into the ipsilateral subthalamic nucleus. We performed various behavioral tests to evaluate the changes in levodopa-induced dyskinesias after optogenetic expression and illumination in the subthalamic nucleus. RESULTS: The behavioral tests revealed that optical inhibition of the subthalamic nucleus significantly ameliorated levodopa-induced dyskinesia by reducing the duration of the [[grafo/termos/dyskinesia|dyskinesias]] as well as the severity of axial dyskinesia. CONCLUSIONS: These findings will provide a useful foundation for the future development of optogenetic modulation systems that could be considered as an approach to dyskinesia therapy.
