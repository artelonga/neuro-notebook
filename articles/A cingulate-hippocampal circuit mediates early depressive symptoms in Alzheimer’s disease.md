Abstract Depressive symptoms are prevalent and precedes [[grafo/termos/cognitive decline|cognitive decline]] in Alzheimer’s disease (AD), which worsen the clinical outcome and severely challenge the life quality of the patients. However, the [[grafo/termos/neural circuitry|neural circuitry]] mediating mood disturbance in AD remains elusive. Here we report that the glutamatergic projection from the [[grafo/termos/anterior cingulate cortex|anterior cingulate cortex]] (ACC) to the ventral [[grafo/termos/hippocampal ca1|hippocampal CA1]] (vCA1) acts as the [[grafo/termos/neural substrates|neural substrate]] of depressive symptoms in AD. We systemically mapped axonal projection in [[grafo/termos/early stages|early stage]] of 5xFAD mice (3 months), which accompanies depressive- like behavior in these animals and identified reduced axonal projection from ACC to vCA1. This is in accordance with reduced axonal calcium signals detected with in vivo [[grafo/termos/fiber photometry|fiber photometry]]. [[grafo/termos/chemogenetics|Chemogenetic]] or optogenetic reversal of ACC-vCA1 circuitry activity efficiently ameliorated the depressive-like behaviors as well as [[grafo/termos/cognitive impairment|cognitive impairment]] in 5xFAD mice. We further identified the synaptic molecules neuregulin-1 (Nrg1) as one vital signal regulating the synaptic [[grafo/termos/glutamate|glutamate]] transmission from the ACC to the vCA1. Collectively, these results indicated ACC-vCA1 as a critical pathway that regulates emotional states in Alzheimer’ disease. Targeting cingulate cortices with [[grafo/termos/brain stimulation|brain stimulation]] may treat [[grafo/termos/depression|depression]] in AD.
