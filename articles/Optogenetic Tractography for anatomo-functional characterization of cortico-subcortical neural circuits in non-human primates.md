Dissecting [[grafo/termos/neural circuitry|neural circuitry]] in [[grafo/termos/nonhuman primates|non-human primates]] (NHP) is crucial to identify potential [[grafo/termos/neuromodulation|neuromodulation]] anatomical targets for the treatment of pharmacoresistant neuropsychiatric diseases by electrical neuromodulation. How targets of [[grafo/termos/deep brain stimulation|deep brain stimulation]] (DBS) and cortical targets of [[grafo/termos/transcranial magnetic stimulation|transcranial magnetic stimulation]] (TMS) compare and might complement one another is an important question. Combining optogenetics and tractography may enable anatomo-functional characterization of large brain cortico-subcortical neural pathways. For the proof-of-concept this approach was used in the NHP brain to characterize the motor cortico-subthalamic pathway (m_CSP) which might be involved in DBS action mechanism in Parkinson's disease (PD). Rabies-G-pseudotyped and Rabies-G-VSVg-pseudotyped EIAV lentiviral vectors encoding the opsin [[grafo/termos/channelrhodopsin-2|ChR2]] gene were stereotaxically injected into the [[grafo/termos/subthalamic nucleus|subthalamic nucleus]] (STN) and were retrogradely transported to the layer of the [[grafo/termos/motor cortex|motor cortex]] projecting to STN. A precise anatomical mapping of this pathway was then performed using histology-guided high angular resolution MRI tractography guiding accurately cortical photostimulation of m_CSP origins. Photoexcitation of m_CSP [[grafo/termos/axon terminals|axon terminals]] or m_CSP cortical origins modified the spikes distribution for photosensitive STN neurons [[grafo/termos/firing rate|firing rate]] in non-equivalent ways. Optogenetic tractography might help design preclinical neuromodulation studies in NHP models of neuropsychiatric disease choosing the most appropriate target for the tested hypothesis.
